The problem

Writing the plan down was the easy part.

You have a WISP, or you know you’re supposed to. A $29 template, or the IRS’s free one, covers the “written” part. It can’t show anyone that the plan is actually working.

  • Your insurer asks

    “Is MFA on for every account? Is every laptop encrypted?”

    You tick “yes” and sign. If you ever make a claim, that signature matters, so you want to know it’s true.

  • A client asks

    “How do you keep my Social Security number safe?”

    A real answer they can check beats a reassuring email.

  • A laptop goes missing

    “Was it encrypted?”

    On that day, what matters is whether you can show it was encrypted when it was taken. The answer can change what you have to report, and to whom.

What a template says

“All company laptops use full-disk encryption.”

A promise. Nobody can check it, not even you.

What we watch

The protections that matter most, checked rather than assumed.

Your founding-pilot assessment looks at all nine once. From launch, your plan watches them every day and tells you in one sentence when something slips.

  • Passkeys and MFA

    A second sign-in check on every account, and a nudge toward phishing-resistant passkeys, which a fake login page can’t trick out of you.

  • Disk encryption

    BitLocker on every computer, so a lost or stolen laptop doesn’t hand over client files.

  • Updates

    Windows and key software kept current. We flag anything that has been waiting too long.

  • Remote-access exposure

    From outside, we look for exposed remote desktop and known, actively exploited flaws in your firewall or VPN. We also list the remote-control tools installed inside.

  • Backups, tested

    A quarterly restore drill shows your backups actually come back, with a signed record of what was restored and when.

  • Verify before you pay

    A request to change bank details, send a refund or wire money gets a call-back to the number on file and a second approver. A cloned voice can fake the boss, but it can’t fake the call-back.

  • Mailbox takeover

    We alert you when an account suddenly forwards mail outside the firm, signs in from an unusual country, adds a new login method or gains a new admin.

  • Tamper alarm

    Each computer checks in with a signed heartbeat. If it goes quiet or Defender is switched off, you hear from our servers, which an attacker on your computer doesn’t control.

  • Client numbers left in the open

    The Kovyr app finds Social Security and card numbers sitting unencrypted in documents and helps you encrypt them. It reports counts, never the numbers.

Why it can’t be quietly faked

Think of a notary’s numbered ledger, kept in public. Every page is stamped by an outside clock and countersigned by independent witnesses, so nobody, including us, can tear out or rewrite a page without it showing.

Each entry carries the fingerprint of the one before it. If anyone removes or edits an entry, the chain breaks in plain view.

  1. Signed at the source

    Each computer signs its own results with a key locked inside its security chip.

  2. Stamped by outside clocks

    Two independent timestamp services mean nobody can backdate a good day.

  3. Written to a public log

    The log is append-only and countersigned by outside witnesses. Missing days show up as gaps.

  4. Checked by anyone

    A free, open-source verifier does the checking, with no account and no need to trust us.

FAQ

Straight answers

Does this make me FTC compliant?

No product can do that on its own, and we won’t claim to. The FTC Safeguards Rule asks you to run an information security program, and a written plan is part of that. Kovyr Proof helps you run the technical parts and keep evidence that they’re working.

It isn’t legal advice, and it isn’t a guarantee. For questions about your obligations, talk to your attorney or compliance adviser.

What leaves my computers?

Your signed records hold only yes/no answers and counts (like “12 of 12 laptops encrypted”), time-stamped. To send you useful alerts, we also see your staff’s work account names and computer names (so we can say “Maria’s laptop encryption is off”). Never file names, documents, emails or client information.

The public log that makes your records checkable holds only a scrambled fingerprint of each record, not the record itself. Nobody can read your results there; they stay private until you choose to share a Proof Pack.

Do you see my clients’ data?

No. When the Kovyr app looks for Social Security or card numbers sitting unencrypted in documents, it shows you where they are on your own computer. We only ever see the count, never the numbers or the documents.

Do I need an IT person?

No. Most fixes are settings in tools you already have, and we give you one plain-English task at a time with a short “why this matters” note. If something truly needs hands-on IT work, we’ll say so plainly.

What if I already have an IT provider?

Good, because we work with them. They make the fixes, and we show what needs doing and prove when it’s done. We don’t sell managed IT, so we’re not after their job.

Will it lower my insurance premium?

We can’t promise that, and we won’t. What it gives you is evidence. Instead of just ticking “yes” on an application, you can show your broker exactly which protections were on, and when. Whether an insurer gives you credit for that is up to the insurer.

What computers are supported?

Windows computers and Microsoft 365 come first. Mac support follows after launch, and Google Workspace after that. If your office runs mostly on Macs or Google, tell us, because it helps us decide what to build next.

What does “can’t be quietly faked” actually mean?

Each record is signed on the computer that made it, time-stamped by independent outside clocks and written to a public, append-only log. If anyone, including us, later changed, backdated or deleted a record, the verifier would show it.

It proves what was checked and when. It doesn’t prove a firm can’t be hacked, and we never claim it does.
