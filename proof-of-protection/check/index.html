Can someone send email pretending to be your firm?

Fake and hijacked email is how most small offices lose money: a "new bank details" message that looks like it came from you or someone you trust. See how well your domain blocks impersonation, in plain English, with the exact fixes.

For example jane@smithcpa.com or smithcpa.com. We only use the part after the @.

Private by design. This check runs in your browser. It asks a public DNS service (Cloudflare) for your domain's public records. Kovyr doesn't see or store what you type unless you choose to send us your results. If Cloudflare doesn't answer, your browser asks Google Public DNS instead. No cookies, no analytics.

Checking…

  • Mail servers
  • Approved senders (SPF)
  • Spoofing policy (DMARC)
  • Email signing (DKIM)
  • Encrypted delivery (optional)

What this check looks at

SPF
Your domain's list of servers allowed to send its email.
DMARC
Your instruction to Gmail, Outlook and others: deliver, quarantine or reject email that fails the checks. This is what stops fakes using your exact address.
DKIM
A signature on your outgoing email. We look under common names only, so "not found" is not a failure.
MTA-STS and TLS-RPT
Optional extras that keep email to you encrypted in transit and report problems.

What it can't tell you

  • Whether a mailbox has already been taken over. A criminal inside a real account sends from your real address, and no DNS record stops that. Kovyr Proof watches for mailbox takeover and alerts you.
  • Lookalike domains, such as smithcpa-co.com. Those belong to someone else, so your records can't block them. A call-back to a known number before any change to payment details can.
  • Everything else that stops losses, like passkeys, encrypted laptops and tested backups. That's what the full Proof Check covers.
