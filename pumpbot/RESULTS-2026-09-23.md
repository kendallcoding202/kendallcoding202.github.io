## Q1b / Q2 — which trail level, and first-rung size: UNDERPOWERED, do not act

Only 85 of the 2,896 rows carrying trail data pass the entry filter. Every level and
every first-rung size lands between 0.85x and 0.89x, and every interval overlaps every
other. The sweep cannot distinguish them.

**The pre-registered red flag did NOT fire**, which is the one encouraging result here:
the optimum is interior (20–35%), not monotone down to the tightest level. The tick-time
measurement is behaving sanely where the old look-ahead-biased one did not. It is thin,
not broken.

At ~3% of rows passing the filter, a usable sample is roughly a fortnight away.

## Q3 — fill feasibility: NOT ANSWERED

The checkpoint columns shipped empty. `finalize()` discards `pathPrices` and writes
`pathMultiples`; the export read the field name off the collecting code rather than the
writing code. `hasPathData` read 0 on all 40,346 rows, which is exactly what that flag
was added to catch, and it caught it in the first minute. Fixed; the data is on disk and
the next export carries it.

## Q4 — Mayhem: no support for the hypothesis

`f_launchVTokens` is unimodal (~1.03–1.07e9, 686 distinct values), so the extra billion
tokens are held off-curve and there is **no structural label** — as pre-registered, that
means the question cannot be settled from this data. On the flag we do have, Mayhem rows
score 0.9469 [0.918, 0.989] against 0.9154 for the rest: slightly BETTER, not worse.
Nothing supports "the edge is just an agent detector". Filter-passing Mayhem rows are
n=27, below the pre-registered floor of 30, so that cut is not reported as a result.

## What this means

The edge measured all week was the impossible rows. What is left is a filter that
demonstrably sorts good launches from bad ones and still cannot clear costs.

Do not put real money in. Do not tune the ladder — the thing to establish is whether any
edge exists at all, and no exit schedule fixes a book that starts at break-even before
the trail correction.

The two things that would change this picture: the root cause of the impossible prices
(still unknown, still occurring), and the fill probe, which is the only measurement that
can say whether even a break-even book survives contact with real execution.

# Root cause: it is Mayhem, and the curve maths does not apply to it

Found after the run above, by asking what the impossible rows have in common.

| | rate |
|---|---|
| Mayhem base rate, rows with known launch reserves (n=2,673) | 32.1% |
| Mayhem share of rows priced below their own launch price (n=782) | 69.9% |
| **Mayhem share of impossible outcomes (n=14)** | **100%** |
| Mayhem rows priced below their own launch price (n=859) | **63.7%** |
| non-Mayhem rows (n=1,814) | 13.0% |

**The mechanism.** Mayhem mints an extra 1,000,000,000 tokens OUTSIDE the bonding curve.
Selling them into it pushes `vTokens` above the launch supply, so the price falls below
the launch price — something a closed curve cannot do, since its own tokens can only be
bought back out. Buying them back does the reverse and can carry the price far past where
a closed curve would have completed.

Constant product is not being violated. What is not conserved is the TOKEN SUPPLY IN THE
CURVE, and every bound derived this week assumed it was:

- the ~15x appreciation ceiling
- the ~115 SOL reserve ceiling
- the launch-price floor

None of them hold for a Mayhem coin. So the "impossible" outcomes are most likely REAL
PRICES on an instrument the model does not describe — not fabricated numbers.

**This does not make them tradeable.** pump.fun's own docs warn that once the agent is a
net seller its extra billion tokens are in circulation and "there may be some holders who
cannot sell their tokens into the bonding curve due to the lack of liquidity". A real
price you cannot exit at is still not profit, and that is precisely what the paper book
cannot tell apart.

**A structural Mayhem tell, at last.** Q4 asked for a label that does not depend on
catching the agent trade inside a 30-second window, and looked for it in launch supply —
which is unimodal, because the extra tokens are minted off-curve. The tell is the
BEHAVIOUR, not the launch state: a price below the coin's own launch price implies
out-of-band token flow. 63.7% of flagged Mayhem rows show it against 13.0% of the rest,
and some of that 13% will be Mayhem the 30-second window missed.

**What it revises.** Excluding those rows from a curve replay stays correct — the replay
models a closed curve and these are not one. But the reason changes, and with it the
conclusion: this is not a decoding bug quietly corrupting the whole dataset. It is one
identifiable population the maths does not cover. The non-Mayhem book is unaffected, and
1.0102x [0.995, 1.046] stands as the honest read of it.

Caveat, stated plainly: the 100% figure is n=14. The 63.7% against 13.0% is n=859 against
n=1,814 and is not in doubt.
