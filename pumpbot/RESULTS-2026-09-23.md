### CORRECTION (added after re-checking on the full population)

That sentence was true of the 83 covered rows and I wrote it as though it were true of the
analysis. It is not. On all 1,388 filter-passing rows, which is what every headline figure
here is computed over, the shipped replay is **monotone**:

| trail | full 1,388 (94% inferred) | the 83 covered rows |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 1.2440 [1.194, 1.294] | 0.8709 [0.831, 0.913] |
| 25% | 1.1538 [1.109, 1.198] | **0.9039** [0.848, 0.968] |
| 50% | 0.9980 [0.962, 1.035] | 0.8658 [0.820, 0.919] |

Tighter is always better, all the way down — which is precisely the signature the
pre-registration named as "something in the measurement is wrong, not a 10% trail is
optimal". Only 83 of 1,388 rows carry a measurement, so the other 94% fall through to the
inference, and the inference was corrected for its FILL and explicitly not for its
TRIGGER: it is still handed the global peak.

**What this costs the headline.** Every figure in this file uses the configured 50% trail,
where 94% of rows are inferred. At that level the inference gives 0.9980 and the measured
subset gives 0.8658. The gap is the inference's remaining optimism, and it is large enough
that the true value cannot be pinned from this export — it is somewhere between, closer to
the top only if the 83 covered rows are unrepresentative.

So the honest position on exits is stronger than "underpowered": **no trail level can be
chosen from this data, and the ~1.00x headline is optimistic by an unknown amount.**
Strict mode (refusing to infer) is not a way out either — it keeps every row that never
reached the first rung, because such a row never consults the trail, so it drops only
rung-reaching rows and thereby drops winners selectively. It reports 0.7115-0.7146 on
n=890, which is a winner-depleted sample and a floor rather than an estimate.

The fix is coverage, and coverage is just time: trail recording began 2026-09-22 21:47.

At ~3% of rows passing the filter, a usable sample is roughly a fortnight away.

# Root cause: it is Mayhem, and the curve maths does not apply to it

Found after the run above, by asking what the impossible rows have in common.

| | rate |
|---|---|
| Mayhem base rate, rows with known launch reserves (n=2,673) | 32.1% |
| Mayhem share of rows priced below their own launch price (n=782) | 69.9% |
| **Mayhem share of impossible outcomes (n=14)** | **100%** |
| Mayhem rows priced below their own launch price (n=859) | **63.7%** |
| non-Mayhem rows (n=1,814) | 13.0% |

**The mechanism.** Mayhem mints an extra 1,000,000,000 tokens OUTSIDE the bonding curve.
Selling them into it pushes `vTokens` above the launch supply, so the price falls below
the launch price — something a closed curve cannot do, since its own tokens can only be
bought back out. Buying them back does the reverse and can carry the price far past where
a closed curve would have completed.

Constant product is not being violated. What is not conserved is the TOKEN SUPPLY IN THE
CURVE, and every bound derived this week assumed it was:

- the ~15x appreciation ceiling
- the ~115 SOL reserve ceiling
- the launch-price floor

None of them hold for a Mayhem coin. So the "impossible" outcomes are most likely REAL
PRICES on an instrument the model does not describe — not fabricated numbers.

**This does not make them tradeable.** pump.fun's own docs warn that once the agent is a
net seller its extra billion tokens are in circulation and "there may be some holders who
cannot sell their tokens into the bonding curve due to the lack of liquidity". A real
price you cannot exit at is still not profit, and that is precisely what the paper book
cannot tell apart.

**A structural Mayhem tell, at last.** Q4 asked for a label that does not depend on
catching the agent trade inside a 30-second window, and looked for it in launch supply —
which is unimodal, because the extra tokens are minted off-curve. The tell is the
BEHAVIOUR, not the launch state: a price below the coin's own launch price implies
out-of-band token flow. 63.7% of flagged Mayhem rows show it against 13.0% of the rest,
and some of that 13% will be Mayhem the 30-second window missed.

**What it revises.** Excluding those rows from a curve replay stays correct — the replay
models a closed curve and these are not one. But the reason changes, and with it the
conclusion: this is not a decoding bug quietly corrupting the whole dataset. It is one
identifiable population the maths does not cover. The non-Mayhem book is unaffected, and
1.0102x [0.995, 1.046] stands as the honest read of it.

Caveat, stated plainly: the 100% figure is n=14. The 63.7% against 13.0% is n=859 against
n=1,814 and is not in doubt.

# Root cause of the impossible prices: found

Q0 asked how many outcomes were physically impossible. It did not ask why. The answer
changes which rows have to be excluded, and it is not the ones the ceiling catches.

**Nothing impossible ever happened to the price. The denominator was deflated.**

| | impossible (108) | normal |
|---|---|---|
| `f_mayhem` | 1 | 0 |
| `f_agentBuyShare` | 0.634 | 0 |
| entry market cap | **9.84 SOL** | 28.14 SOL |
| **peak market cap** | **37.66 SOL** | 31.99 SOL |

A peak of 37.7 SOL is unremarkable against the ~115 a curve completes at. Mayhem mints
about 1e9 tokens off-curve, `vTokens` jumps, and since `price = vSol/vTokens` the observed
price collapses to roughly a third of a true launch. We record that as our entry and
measure every later multiple against it. The 46x and the 228x were ordinary coins divided
by a number that was wrong at the moment we looked.

## The ceiling was the wrong exclusion

It drops 108 rows and leaves 845 whose entry is still deflated (20.8 vs 28.1 SOL):

| | mean peak | mean end |
|---|---|---|
| mayhem (845) | 1.7386x | 0.7728x |
| clean (39,393) | 1.2719x | 0.9388x |

Inflated on peak, far worse on outcome — the shape that drags anything tuned on peaks
toward the coins that end worst. `analyze()` now excludes on `features.mayhem`, on
measurement validity: a multiple with a broken denominator is not evidence either way.

It does **not** rescue the book. Only 27 of 341 traded rows were mayhem, because entry
already rejects them; replaying the shipped strategy over the 346 trades actually taken
gives 0.9644x before the correction and 0.9637x after.

## Costs are not the problem

Gross is **0.9637x [0.891, 1.047]** — below 1.0 before any cost is applied. Take the
optimistic case: slip is 0.4% rather than the modelled 2%, and the ATA rent is recovered.
Costs fall 11.55pp to ~5.6pp. Then the point estimate is 0.910x and **the top of the 95%
interval is 0.988x**. Even the optimistic end of the interval, with both cost fixes
shipped, still loses. Cost reduction cannot save a book that is under water before costs.

## No entry signal in the features we collect

39,393 clean scorable rows, split 60/40 in time, one pre-committed threshold per feature
(the median, so nothing is tuned), 29 feature/side splits:

- baseline out-of-sample **0.9019x**
- best split out-of-sample **0.9156x** (`f_netVolumeSol <= 0.0494`)
- needed to clear best-case costs **1.06x**

Not one split is profitable, in or out of sample. Every leading split points the same way
— low volume, few buyers, few buys — which describes where losses are smaller, not an
edge. The shipped filter already combines these and reaches 0.9637x, better than any
single feature and still losing. The ceiling of this approach looks like ~0.96-0.97
against a ~1.06 requirement.

# The horizon hypothesis: also negative

The last live idea was that the 15-minute window was too short. A fresh export carries
`multAt2s … multAt900s`, so it can be tested directly.

Coverage decays hard — 6,723 rows at 2s, 4,226 at 60s, 1,263 at 300s, **13** at 900s — and
comparing means across horizons with different populations is a selection artifact, so
every curve below is computed on a FIXED population present at all listed checkpoints.

Clean population (ceiling + mayhem excluded), n=3,593:

| horizon | mean | median | share >1.06 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2s | 0.9816 | 1.0000 | 8.9% |
| 5s | 0.9730 | 1.0000 | 10.6% |
| 10s | 0.9702 | 1.0000 | 12.1% |
| 30s | 0.9654 | 1.0000 | 13.8% |
| 60s | **0.9430** | 1.0000 | 13.2% |

**Monotone decay. Every horizon is below 1.0, and holding longer is strictly worse.**

The unfiltered version of the same table looks flat at ~1.00-1.01 and invites the opposite
conclusion. The difference is 633 rows, 15% of the population, whose mean at 60s is
**1.4196** — the deflated-denominator artifact again. Without today's exclusion this
analysis would have read as "roughly break-even, maybe something at 60s".

Two mechanistic notes: 29.3% of paths never move at any checkpoint, and the median is
1.0000 at every horizon out to 30s. Most launches simply do not trade after entry. That is
not a strategy that needs tuning; it is an absence of follow-through.

## Where this leaves the question

Across everything measured:

- gross strategy on trades actually taken: **0.9637x**, below 1.0 before any cost
- best of 29 out-of-sample feature splits: **0.9156x**, against a 1.06x requirement
- best horizon in the observable range: **0.9816x at 2s**, decaying with time
- cost floor even with both fixes shipped: **~5.6pp**

Every apparent edge this project has measured has turned out to be an artifact, and each
correction has removed it rather than reduced it. Taker-side sniping of pump.fun launches,
as instrumented here, does not clear costs at any entry threshold or holding period we can
observe. Continuing to tune inside this space is not the bottleneck; the space is.
