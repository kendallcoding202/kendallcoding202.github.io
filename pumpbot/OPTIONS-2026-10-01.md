# Where else Jev, or a bot, could find an edge — 2026-10-01

Ranked by how good the reason to expect an edge is, not by how exciting it sounds.
Context: $200 of capital, an automated bot, a retail connection, Jev available.

## Worth testing

### 0. Polymarket temperature markets vs a real weather forecast — TESTED 2026-10-02: NO (`PREREG-WEATHER.md`)

The Jev-on-Polymarket run (void, `PREREG-JEV-POLYMARKET.md`) showed that about **30% of
usable Polymarket markets are daily temperature buckets** ("highest temperature in Seoul
be 35°C"), resolving every day. The market's Brier on them was 0.121; Jev's, reading only
the question, 0.163. The market wins because it prices in forecasts.

The question: does a **calibrated forecast model** (ensemble forecasts as they were
issued, from Open-Meteo's historical-forecast archive) beat the market price, after
fees, 24 hours out?
- **For:** hundreds of markets a day, small and thin; fees are low far from 50%;
  forecasts are free; outcomes are objective; the archive allows a clean backtest
  (forecasts as issued at the time, so no hindsight).
- **Against:** this is a known approach and bots already trade it; each market resolves
  on one specific weather station, and the forecast has to target that station.
- **Needs** on the allowlist: `historical-forecast-api.open-meteo.com`,
  `ensemble-api.open-meteo.com`, `api.open-meteo.com`, `archive-api.open-meteo.com`.

### 1. Jev forecasting prediction markets — TESTED 2026-10-01: void, leans NO

Jev blind gave incoherent Yes/No answers and lost to the market wherever it was scored.
Kept below for the record.

Jev is a probability forecaster. A prediction market is a price that *is* a probability.
So the test is direct: is Jev's probability closer to the outcome than the market's
price? Buy the side Jev thinks is underpriced.

- **Why it might work:** many small markets have few traders and prices that go stale.
  Big markets (elections, the Fed) will not be beaten; the long tail might.
- **Why it might not:** markets are usually well calibrated, and published research on
  language-model forecasters generally finds them near the crowd, not above it.
- **Fits $200:** yes. Positions can be a few dollars, and fees are small or zero on
  many markets.
- **Testable cheaply:** Polymarket publishes price histories of resolved markets. The
  same hindsight check as `PREREG-JEV-NEWS.md` is needed: Jev must not already know the
  outcomes, so only markets resolving after its knowledge ends can count.
- **Needs:** `gamma-api.polymarket.com`, `clob.polymarket.com` and
  `api.elections.kalshi.com` added back to the network allowlist.
- **Note:** our earlier Kalshi/Polymarket work tested *arbitrage* only (rejected). This
  is a different question that has not been asked.

## Real, but small at $200

### Funding-rate carry (Hyperliquid)

Hold the coin and short the same amount in perpetual futures; collect the funding that
long traders pay. It is not a prediction; it is being paid to take the other side.
Historically around 10–20% a year in good periods, close to zero or negative in bad ones.
On $200 that is **roughly $2–3 a month at best**, with exchange risk. It is honest
money, but not a fix for anything.

## The arithmetic that applies to all of it

Even a strategy that really works earns a percentage of the money in it. At $200:

| weekly return | per month |
|---|---|
| 0.5% (very good for a real edge) | ~$4 |
| 2% (exceptional; rarely lasts) | ~$17 |

And a strategy with no proven edge has a negative expected value after costs. Nothing on
this list should be funded until it has passed its registered test and two weeks of
paper trading.

## Exploratory, 2026-10-01 — Polymarket's own calibration (not registered; a lead only)

7,424 resolved markets priced 24h before close, outcomes known. No forecasts used; the
weather test's forecasts were not looked at.

- **Prices are well calibrated overall.** Weather markets almost exactly (price 0.145 →
  14.7% Yes; 0.417 → 42.0%).
- **The classic long-shot bias is absent.** Selling 2–10¢ contracts loses: −1.8% per
  dollar, interval [−2.8%, −0.9%].
- **One lead: cheap sports contracts paid more often than priced.** 497 sports markets at
  2–20¢ averaged 8.3¢ and resolved Yes 12.9% of the time. Buying them: +27% per dollar at
  a 1¢ half-spread, but the 95% interval is [−1%, +59%], and +12% [−13%, +39%] at 2¢.
  It is **not significant**, and it was one of eight rules tried.
- **The likely artifact:** the "price" is the last hourly trade, not an ask you could
  buy at. In thin, cheap markets a stale last trade can sit well below the real ask,
  which would produce exactly this pattern. Only live order books can settle it.

**Next step if pursued:** a forward paper test on live order books. Each hour, record the
best ask and depth on sports contracts at 2–20¢, settle each at resolution, and register
the rule before the first record.
