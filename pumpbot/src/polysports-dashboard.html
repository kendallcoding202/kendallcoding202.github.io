How this test works

Every 15 minutes the tracker reads every Polymarket sports market that ends in about a day, and records a paper bet at the real asking price, plus Polymarket's fee, when a $2 order would fill at 2¢–20¢. No order is ever sent. There are two separate tests:

Test A buys YES on Yes/No markets ("Will X win?", "Exact score 2–1?"). Today these are all soccer.
Test B buys the cheap side of two-way markets: over/unders, spreads, team totals, corners and team-vs-team moneylines (NFL, NHL, soccer…).

When the game is decided, each bet is marked settled. A share pays $1 if the side bought wins, nothing if it loses.

What each bet is: the green-blue label says what was bought. BET YES means the question has to come true; BET OVER 61.5 means more than 61.5 points; BET North Carolina −17.5 means they win by 18 or more; BET Wagner to win means Wagner wins. A share costs the price shown and pays $1 if the bet hits, so $2 at 10¢ buys 20 shares and pays $20.

Running result and verdict: profit is shown as it comes in. The test stops at 400 settled bets and at least 7 days, or at 28 days, and the verdict is taken then, once, from the bets settled by that moment. The rules were written down before the first bet (PREREG-POLY-SPORTS.md) and do not change with the running number.

The verdict is YES only if profit per dollar is above zero and stays above zero across the plausible range (the 95% interval, resampling whole games). Otherwise it is NO. Each test has its own stop and its own verdict.

Watch follows hockey and college football games that kick off too soon for the tests (less than 22 hours away) — in practice the day's games the tests never saw. Same rule, same real prices and fees; its wins and losses show as they come in because it decides nothing.

By sport and bet type: counts show from the start; each slice's record shows at its test's stop. These slices are a breakdown, not separate verdicts — with a dozen slices, one will look good by luck alone. A slice that looks good becomes a new idea to test forward, on bets not seen yet.
