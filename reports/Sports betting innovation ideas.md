# Build for the bettor, not the house

The best twist open to a solo developer in November 2026 is not a new way to wager. It is the first product that sits plainly on the bettor's side: an app that puts a dollar price tag on any bet slip, then grows into one budget and tax ledger across every sportsbook and prediction market a person uses. The second-best build is a no-money friends league in which the job of bookmaker rotates around the group. Both follow from the same two findings. First, every route to handling real stakes is closed to an individual: sportsbook licences cost $50,000 to $25 million per state, sweepstakes models are banned by statute in about ten states, Stripe and Apple block paid-entry contests, and Kalshi's API terms forbid third-party trading front ends. Second, the unmet needs that remain are ones the incumbents have no incentive to fix: bettors cannot see what a parlay costs them, their limits and records are scattered across apps, and the most common way Americans bet (with friends) has no good product. Both recommended builds need no gambling licence, earn money through subscriptions, and can launch on the web before facing App Review. The main caveats are that the "nobody does this yet" judgment rests on searches that could not reach Reddit or every small app, and that none of these ideas has been tested with real bettors. This report is research, not legal advice.

## Bettors cannot see the price, and their friends are not in the app

The loudest complaints about sportsbooks are operational, but the deepest one is about price. An analysis of one-to-three-star reviews for the five big apps ranked slow withdrawals first (24%), accounts limited after winning second (18%), and same-game-parlay pricing third (15%), followed by live-betting freezes, promo bait-and-switch and identity-check loops ([Unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/draftkings-fanduel-betmgm-caesars-sports-betting-apps-ranked-2026)). That source is a low-transparency aggregator, so treat the percentages as directional. The pricing complaint, though, has hard numbers behind it. In Maryland, **parlays were 38.5% of handle but 68.5% of sportsbook revenue, with a hold of 18.8% against 5.7% on single bets** ([CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2026/08/18/polymarket-launching-parlays-in-us-as-kalshi-looks-to-cash-in-on-combos/)). The bettor never sees that number. Nothing on a slip states the house's cut, and the same parlay can be priced very differently from one book to the next ([Juice Reel](https://juicereel.beehiiv.com/p/same-game-parlays-are-mispriced-everywhere)).

Prediction markets were supposed to fix this, and on single games they often do. On parlays the evidence conflicts. One benchmark put the bettor's loss at about 19 cents per dollar on a sportsbook parlay against 8 to 9 cents on Kalshi combos ([Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/prediction-markets-take-bigger-slice-of-us-sports-betting-as-world-cup-drives-volume-surge/)), while a Citizens and Juice Reel study found combos lose bettors more than sportsbook parlays do ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/prediction-market-combos-bigger-losers-for-bettors-than-sportsbook-parlays/)). Bloomberg counted **$294 million in net retail losses on Kalshi combos in 2026** ([Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/prediction-markets-take-bigger-slice-of-us-sports-betting-as-world-cup-drives-volume-surge/)). If analysts cannot agree which venue is cheaper, an ordinary bettor holding one specific slip has no chance. No venue yet owns "fair to the casual user."

Account limiting is the best-documented grievance and the least useful one to build on. Massachusetts regulators found that only 0.64% of accounts were limited, but that bettors who beat the closing line were the ones cut while losing players were promoted to VIP ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-prediction-markets-exchanges-sharps-box-september-2025)). It matters as a trust problem more than a feature gap, and the sharp bettors it affects are already moving to exchanges that do not limit.

Casual bettors want something different, and the surveys are consistent about what. They bet for fun (62.7%), to make games more exciting (46.5%) and to compete with friends (35.6%) ([Sacred Heart University poll](https://www.sacredheart.edu/media/shu-media/press-room-2026/SHU-2026-Nationwide-Sports-Gambling-Poll-Report-ADA.pdf)). Stakes are small: FanDuel says its **median bet is $5 and its median bettor loses $63 a month** ([Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/fanduel-push-backs-on-high-roller-image-median-bet-is-just-5)). The single largest betting behavior in the country is not on an app at all. **Fifteen percent of adults bet with friends or family in the past year, against 10% who bet online** ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/02/americans-increasingly-see-legal-sports-betting-as-a-bad-thing-for-society-and-sports/)). The incumbent product is still a solitary slip against the house. When Kalshi removed parlay sharing in an August 2026 update, its app rating reportedly dropped from 4.05 to 3.0 stars ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/articles/app-updates/kalshi-v9-9-113-removes-parlay-sharing-and-btc-chart-data-in-august-2026-update-causing-rating-drop)).

The third need is protection from the product itself, and here the trend is sharp. Among online bettors, 60% have chased a loss (up from 52% a year earlier) and 15% have called a helpline or sought help (up from 9%) ([Siena / St. Bonaventure](https://www.sbu.edu/news/2026/04/13/siena-sbu-survey--over-half-of-men-18-49-have-sportsbook-accounts)). A U.S. News survey found 51% of bettors wager to pay bills ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/more-than-half-of-us-sports-bettors-wager-to-pay-bills-survey-finds-sept-22-2026)). **Among men under 30, the share calling legal betting bad for society jumped from 22% to 47% in three years** ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/02/americans-increasingly-see-legal-sports-betting-as-a-bad-thing-for-society-and-sports/)). The heaviest users are the ones souring fastest. Yet the existing safety tools fail in a predictable way: bettors endorse limits in surveys, rarely turn them on ([JMIR Formative Research](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e101430)), and can dodge any limit by opening another app. Meanwhile operators are pointing AI the other way. FanDuel's assistant exists to help bettors build and place wagers faster ([CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2026/06/03/sportsbooks-integrating-ai-tools-bettors-which-chatbot-is-the-best-tipster/)). The research found no product using AI to check a price, hold a budget or explain a bet's true cost.

Two demographic facts round out the picture. Online betting among women under 30 rose from 6% to 16% between 2022 and 2025 ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/02/americans-increasingly-see-legal-sports-betting-as-a-bad-thing-for-society-and-sports/)), and women who bet on non-sports events favor award shows and reality TV ([Sacred Heart University poll](https://www.sacredheart.edu/media/shu-media/press-room-2026/SHU-2026-Nationwide-Sports-Gambling-Poll-Report-ADA.pdf)). And 74% of bettors aged 18 to 34 say they are more invested in individual plays and players than in who wins the game (same poll), which favors moment-and-player formats.

One limit on all of this: the researchers could not open Reddit, Trustpilot or X directly, and no survey asks casual bettors what features they wish existed. The wants above are inferred from complaints, motivations and behavior.

## Nearly every clever format has a tombstone or a billion-dollar owner

The first lesson from the competitive record is that being a betting venue is a capital war. FanDuel and DraftKings take roughly three-quarters of online sportsbook revenue ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/sports-betting-statistics-2026-us-handle-revenue-by-state)). ESPN put its brand and a $2 billion deal behind ESPN Bet, never passed about 2.6% share, and closed it on December 1, 2025, leaving Penn with an $825 million write-down ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/penn-espn-bet-shutdown-thescore-relaunch/)). Fox Bet, WynnBET, Unibet, SI Sportsbook, Betway and Betfred all left for much the same reason: no path to profit below a few points of share ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/sports-betting-graveyard-smaller-companies-exiting-us)).

The second lesson is that the frontier has moved to prediction markets, and that war is even more expensive. Kalshi's monthly volume, counted at $1 face value per contract, climbed from $16.8 billion in May 2026 to **$98.15 billion in October**, about 81% of all US exchange volume, and the company raised money at a $40 billion valuation ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/kalshi-polymarket)). Those notional figures overstate cash staked and are not comparable to sportsbook handle. Still, 15% of Americans and 33% of men aged 18 to 49 have now placed a sports bet through a prediction market ([Siena / St. Bonaventure](https://www.sbu.edu/news/2026/04/13/siena-sbu-survey--over-half-of-men-18-49-have-sportsbook-accounts)). DraftKings expects its Predictions product to cost **$650 to $700 million of adjusted earnings in 2026** ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/draftkings-sports-predictions-success-fuels-q3-revenue-growth-nov-5-2026)). A newcomer competing on promotions is competing with that budget.

The third lesson is that every unlicensed wrapper for real-money betting is collapsing into the federal one. Sporttrade shut its state-licensed exchange in June 2026 to seek federal registration ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/263660/sporttrade-exits-betting-with-predictions-applications-pending/)). Novig and ProphetX dropped sweepstakes currencies and relaunched as CFTC-registered exchanges, and Fliff and Rebet have applied to become registered futures brokers ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/sweeps-swaps-fliff-sweepstakes-pivot-prediction-markets/)). "No-vig peer-to-peer exchange" is taken several times over.

The formats that sound most inventive mostly have graves. The table below sorts what was found.

| Format | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed friend-vs-friend betting | Wagr raised $12M, launched in Tennessee in January 2022, shut down within the year | [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2023/04/25/wagr-yahoo-sportsbook-acquisition/) |
| Peer-to-peer pick'em | FanDuel Picks launched April 2025, reached 17 states, closed by May 2026 | [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/03/27/fanduel-picks-shutdown-may-2026/) |
| Consumer skill wagers between friends | Lucra pivoted to selling a white-label kit to venues and brands | [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/lucra-raises-10m-in-funding-to-drive-brand-loyalty-through-gamification) |
| Athlete stock markets | Mojo raised about $100M and abandoned the product; Fantex, Jock MKT and Football Index also failed | [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/bear-market-for-sacks-and-ints-the-difficult-road-of-sports-stock-markets/) |

Splash is the one social format that scaled, and it did so by buying established pool brands and collecting entry fees, which a solo developer cannot process (see the next section).

Tools are where independent builders have won, and that layer is now crowded in its obvious spots. OddsJam's parent sold for up to $160 million on about $26 million of revenue ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2024/12/12/gamblingcom-buying-oddsjam-parent/)), which proves bettors pay for an edge, and a dozen subscription tools at $20 to $80 a month now sell the same odds screens and prop research. In bet tracking, Pikkit reported 600,000 monthly users in 2024 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241209399992/en/Pikkit-Releases-2024-Instant-Replay)) and gives away its core tracker, line shopping and an AI assistant, charging $29.99 a month for closing-line grading and same-game-parlay pricing ([BetSmart](https://www.betsmart.co/tool-reviews/pikkit)). AI pick apps are the most over-supplied corner of all, and the one rigorous test, KellyBench, found that **none of eight frontier AI models turned a profit betting a full Premier League season** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364406/ai-beat-sports-betting-market-top-8-models?amp=1)). On the prediction-market side, one guide counts more than 170 third-party Polymarket tools ([DeFiprime](https://defiprime.com/definitive-guide-to-the-polymarket-ecosystem)).

What the record leaves open is narrower than it first appears. Nobody was found positioning a tool on the casual bettor's side at the moment of purchase. Nobody was found building for the friend group without taking money. And the incumbents best placed to do either (Pikkit, Action Network, OddsJam) are busy extending into prediction markets. Absence from these searches is weak evidence, so a week of hands-on checking in the App Store should precede any build.

## Law, Apple and Stripe leave one lane: no stakes, no orders, no prizes

Gambling in US law is prize plus chance plus consideration, and every workaround removes one element. A solo developer can rely on only one of them. Federal law expressly excludes contests in which players risk nothing but effort or free points supplied by the sponsor ([31 U.S.C. § 5362](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)). The other workarounds are all under state attack. The fantasy carve-out excludes outcomes based on a single team or a single athlete's performance (same statute), so a paid sports-prediction contest does not fit it. California's attorney general called daily fantasy illegal in July 2025 ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/prizepicks-switches-to-peer-to-peer-games-in-california-ahead-of-ag-ruling-july-3-2025)). Skill-contest law varies by state.

Licensing is out of reach. North Carolina charges $1 million to apply for an operator licence ([NC gaming](https://ncgaming.gov/?p=8424)) and New York's initial fee is $25 million ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/online-sports-betting/wagering-tax-rates-us/)). The sweepstakes route is closing by statute. California's AB 831, in force since January 1, 2026, bans dual-currency games that simulate sports wagering and extends criminal liability to payment processors, geolocation vendors and affiliates ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)). New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Montana, Indiana, Maine, Tennessee, Louisiana and Oklahoma have their own bans ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal)).

### Prediction markets are a legal coin flip that a solo developer cannot plug into

The federal regulator backs prediction markets and the courts are split. The Third Circuit held in April 2026 that Kalshi's sports contracts fall under exclusive federal jurisdiction. The Ninth Circuit (August 28) and Sixth Circuit (September 25) held that they are sports bets that states can regulate, and the Ninth Circuit refused a rehearing in a decision reported November 5 ([RotoWire legal timeline](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline); [VitalLaw](https://www.vitallaw.com/news/federal-preemption-9th-cir-ninth-circuit-closes-the-door-on-rehearing-of-kalshi-prediction-markets-ruling/sld0137f603ff1af14c87a178bffdf5e66be5)). Kalshi is geofenced or enjoined in Nevada, Washington and Michigan, Utah has filed criminal charges, and about twenty states are in litigation ([Totalis](https://www.totalis.trade/blogs/prediction-markets/is-kalshi-legal)). New Jersey's Supreme Court petition (No. 26-299) appears to be pending, though trackers disagree on its status and on when Kalshi's response is due ([State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/states-ask-supreme-court-to-clarify-authority-over-prediction-markets/)). Any product whose value depends on sports contracts being available nationwide carries a yes-or-no risk into 2027.

Building on the exchanges is harder than their marketing suggests. Kalshi's Developer Agreement limits API use to "a members own trading" and prohibits facilitating other members' trading or sharing API data with third parties without written permission ([Kalshi Developer Agreement](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf)). Kalshi also announced Builder Codes in December 2025 to let developers "permissionlessly monetize" apps on its liquidity ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-solana-tokenized-predictions)). The research did not reconcile these two documents. The Builder Codes announcement came with Kalshi's tokenized markets on Solana, the agreement is undated, and the fee split is undisclosed, so this is the first question for a lawyer if an exchange-based idea is ever pursued. Separately, anyone who solicits or accepts orders for compensation generally must register as an introducing broker ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260415-want-to-get-into-cftc-regulated-event-contract-markets-heres-how-it-works)). A September 2026 no-action letter exempts "passive" front-end software, but only with a notice filing and with the exchange agreeing to share liability, and never for software that issues buy or sell signals ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/cftc-extends-noaction-relief-for-introducing-broker-registration-to-all-providers-of-passive-software)).

### Apple treats almost anything near betting as gambling

Apple's written rules bar real-money gaming apps without licences and geo-restriction (5.3.4), bar in-app purchase for wagering credit (5.3.3), and say gambling apps should come from "a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer" (5.1.1(ix)) ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Review practice in 2026 goes further, according to developer reports. A points-only football score predictor with no money and no prizes was told in June to declare the Gambling age rating, which means 18+ ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836211)). A free prediction contest with sponsor-funded prizes was rejected in May as real-money gambling ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825845)). A third-party Polymarket front end was rejected in September for lacking licences in every region ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/846498)). These are anecdotes, and trackers and odds apps plainly exist on the store, but the pattern sets the plan: expect an 18+ rating, avoid prizes, and launch on the web first. A paid developer account held as an individual is not enough for anything Apple classes as gambling. Forming an LLC and converting to an organization account is the practical first step. Google Play admits real-money apps only from licensed operators, and Google banned prediction-market Chrome extensions from August 1, 2026 ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032); [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)).

### Payments, affiliates, taxes and data set the business model

Stripe's prohibited list includes sweepstakes and contests, fantasy leagues with prizes, skill tournaments with prizes, entry fees that promise a prize, and "sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary or material prize" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). So the only user payment a solo developer can reliably process is a subscription for software or content, through in-app purchase on iOS and Stripe on the web.

Affiliate income is the other workable stream, with a licensing line through the middle of it. In New Jersey, affiliates paid a flat fee need only a free vendor registration, while those taking a percentage of wagers need a casino-service licence ([NJ DGE](https://www.nj.gov/lps/ge/docs/InternetGaming/AncillaryCompaniesVendor.pdf)). Reported sportsbook bounties cluster around $100 to $350 per first-time depositor, though those bands come from affiliate-software vendors ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/affiliate-commission-rates-benchmark-2026)). Selling picks is legal speech so long as it does not deceive ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/betting/faq/what-are-sports-betting-touts-1d56d433)).

A 2025 tax change creates a new need. Starting with tax year 2026, **gambling losses are deductible only up to 90%**, so a bettor who breaks even can owe tax on a tenth of gross losses ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/obbba-gambling-tax-hike-trump-repeal/)). The first filing season under that rule opens in early 2027. A repeal may intervene: one outlet reports that the House Ways and Means Committee voted in September 2026 to advance a retroactive fix, which is not enactment ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/news/will-the-new-gambling-tax-law-be-changed-in-2027)). How prediction-market gains are taxed was not researched.

Data rules complete the picture. Scraping sportsbook odds breaches the books' terms and is being litigated ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/01/02/swish-oddsjam-lawsuit/)), so odds must come from a licensed feed. The Odds API starts free and costs $30 to $59 a month at indie volumes ([The Odds API](https://the-odds-api.com/)), while prop-level feeds run $99 to $499 a month ([SportsGameOdds](https://sportsgameodds.com/compare)). Player names as statistics are broadly usable. Player images and team logos in promotion are where publicity-rights suits have survived dismissal ([Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/experience/mlbpis-licensing-fight-ends-in-victory-and-settlement-with-draftkings-70248700)).

The net effect is a single lane. A solo developer can ship software that takes no stakes, routes no orders and awards no prizes, charge a subscription for it, and add flat-fee affiliate income one state at a time.

## Eleven white-space ideas, ranked for a builder of one

The ranking below weighs three things: how new the idea is against what the research found, whether one person can build and run it, and how much legal exposure it carries. "Novelty" means not found in this research, which is weaker than proof that it does not exist. The be-the-book mechanic in row 3 is this report's own proposal, built on the survey evidence rather than on any observed product.

| Rank | The twist | Novelty | Solo feasibility | Legal risk | How it earns | What could kill it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bet price tag.** Screenshot any slip before placing it; see the house's cut in dollars and a cheaper place for the same bet | Medium-high: pieces exist inside Pikkit Pro and Juice Reel, but no pre-bet, plain-dollar, bettor's-side product was found | High | Low | Freemium subscription; later, disclosed flat-fee referrals | Pikkit or Action ships it as a feature; pricing correlated same-game parlays is hard |
| 2 | **One budget, every app.** A spending limit and loss ledger across all sportsbooks and exchanges, with a tax export for the 90% rule | Medium-high | Medium: needs imports without account sync | Low | Subscription; seasonal tax-report purchase | Pikkit; repeal of the loss cap; unproven willingness to pay |
| 3 | **Be-the-book friends league.** No-money league where each week one friend sets the lines and the rest bet play bankrolls against them | High for the rotating-bookmaker mechanic; friend-bet ledgers such as WagerLab exist | High | Low to medium: Apple may class it as gambling | Commissioner season pass, ads; weak | Virality; the consumer-social graveyard |
| 4 | **Lock-screen sweat.** Live Activities and watch widgets showing what every open bet needs right now | Medium: no dedicated product surfaced | Medium: live data costs | Low | Subscription | It is a feature; sportsbook apps cover their own bets |
| 5 | **Capper report card.** Neutral, timestamped records for paid pick sellers, graded against closing lines | Medium | High | Low to medium | Buyer subscriptions; badges for honest sellers | Sellers with bad records will not join; DubClub and Juice Reel are close |
| 6 | **Culture pick'em league.** Free friends league on award shows and reality TV, aimed at the fastest-growing cohort | Medium | High | Low to medium | Ads, sponsors; weak | Episodic use; free-to-play apps rarely survive alone |
| 7 | **Sportsbook-to-exchange translator.** Prediction-market prices in American odds, fees included, against sportsbook lines | Medium, and narrowing | Medium-high | Medium: Kalshi's data terms, state litigation | Subscription | Incumbent tools are moving in; the Supreme Court |
| 8 | **Hedge-out calculator.** Use exchange prices to lock profit on sportsbook futures and parlays | Medium | Medium | Medium | Subscription | Thin liquidity; small audience |
| 9 | **What's legal here.** State-by-state tracker of which products work where and what changed this month | Low | High | Low | Search traffic and flat-fee affiliate | Affiliate media covers it piecemeal |
| 10 | **Sponsor-funded free contests** with real prizes | Low | Medium | Medium: official rules, tax forms at $2,000, documented Apple rejections | Sponsors, ads | App Review; Stripe's contest language |

Off the table entirely are a peer-to-peer money app, a sweepstakes sportsbook, paid-entry contests, another athlete stock market, an AI picks app, a generic tracker and a generic odds screen. The first three fail on law and payments. The rest are saturated or have failed repeatedly.

Three judgments drive the order. Ideas 1 and 2 rank highest because they attack the complaint that incumbents are paid not to fix. A sportsbook holding close to 19% on parlays will never print that number on a slip. A tracker that earns referral fees from books has mixed motives too. Idea 3 ranks third despite weak revenue because it targets the largest unserved behavior and is the most genuinely new mechanic on the list. Idea 11 ranks last despite being the most exciting because it is the only one that needs a registered partner, derivatives counsel and a favorable Supreme Court.

## Build the price tag first, then the friends league

### First build: a price tag for any bet, growing into a budget and tax ledger

The product answers one question at the moment it matters: what is this bet costing me? A bettor screenshots a slip before confirming it. The app reads the legs, compares each against the no-vig consensus price across licensed books, and replies in plain terms: the fair payout, the offered payout, and the difference in dollars ("the house keeps about $1.90 of this $10"). It then shows the best available price for the same bet among books legal in the user's state. It never suggests what to bet. That restraint keeps it out of pick-selling and out of the buy-or-sell-signal territory regulators care about, and it is the brand: a second opinion, not a tout.

The first buildable version is a mobile-first web app. It needs screenshot upload with a vision model doing the parsing, which other apps already do: betsAI advertises slip scanning ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756926912)) and Action Network's Playbook turns a screenshot into a bet link ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/?p=212811)). It needs The Odds API for multi-book prices. Scope it to pre-game moneylines, spreads, totals and multi-game parlays in the NFL and NBA, where the math is clean because the legs are independent. Same-game parlays are reportedly the preferred bet of 41% of online bettors ([WSN](https://www.wsn.com/sports-betting-census-2026/)) and the hardest to price, since the legs are correlated. Version one should accept them and show leg-by-leg cost with an honest note that the combined price cannot be verified yet. Version two adds a prop feed and a correlation model. Every checked slip is saved, which builds the second act without extra effort from the user: a running ledger, a monthly budget meter that warns before a self-set limit, and a tax export ready for the first filing season under the 90% rule. An iOS app follows once an LLC and organization account exist, with a share-sheet extension for screenshots and, later, the lock-screen sweat view from idea 4 as the retention feature. Include an 18+ gate, a problem-gambling helpline and a clear "not a sportsbook" statement from day one.

It makes money through a freemium subscription. Give away a few checks a week and charge for unlimited checks, the ledger, budget alerts and the tax export. Price it in single digits per month. The category's tools charge $20 to $80, but they sell to bettors seeking an edge, while this sells to a median bettor losing $63 a month who will not pay half of that for a tool. That pricing point is a judgment, not a tested number. A second stream comes later: a disclosed flat-fee referral when a user taps through to a cheaper book, registered state by state starting with New Jersey's free vendor registration. Avoid revenue-share deals. They require heavier licensing, and they pay the app a percentage of its users' losses, which contradicts the whole position.

The honest risks are three. Pikkit already sells same-game-parlay pricing and line shopping, so the edge is positioning and timing (before the bet, in dollars, for casual bettors) more than capability. Pikkit could copy that framing. The tax hook weakens if Congress restores the full loss deduction. And prediction-market prices should stay out of version one, because Kalshi's terms restrict sharing its API data. Add them later through a licensed data vendor or written permission.

### Second build: a friends league where everyone takes a turn as the house

The product is a season-long league for a group chat. Each member starts with the same play-money bankroll. Each week one member is the book and sets the lines on a short slate of games, while the others bet against those lines. The book's result is the mirror of everyone else's. Standings run all season. No money enters the app, no prizes are awarded, and the app does not help settle side bets.

It targets the right behavior. More Americans bet with friends than on apps, 35.6% of bettors name competing with friends as a motive, and 32.3% cite curiosity about odds and predictions ([Sacred Heart University poll](https://www.sacredheart.edu/media/shu-media/press-room-2026/SHU-2026-Nationwide-Sports-Gambling-Poll-Report-ADA.pdf)). Rotating the bookmaker role also teaches each player how the house's margin works, which fits a market growing wary of the real thing. Legally it sits inside the federal free-points exclusion. The remaining risk is Apple's classification, so it should also start as a web app with invite links. A lawyer should confirm the no-money design, because the research found no source on how ledger-style friend apps are treated.

The first buildable version is small: group creation by link, a weekly slate pulled from the same odds feed, a line-setting screen for the week's book, play-money bet slips, automatic grading from final scores and a standings page. It reuses the data pipeline from the first build.

It earns less. Options are a commissioner season pass for custom rules, history and extra slates (sold as software, never as an entry fee toward a prize), plus advertising. Its real value is distribution. Leagues spread through invitations, which is how pool products grew, and every league member is a candidate for the price-tag app. Rank it second for that reason and for the base rate: standalone social sports apps with far more funding (Buzzer raised $44 million) did not survive, so this should be a cheap experiment and not the main bet.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "What new way to bet can I invent?" has a discouraging answer for an individual, because the law, the app stores and the payment networks have each closed the door on holding stakes, and the companies behind that door are spending hundreds of millions a year. "What does the bettor still lack?" has a much better one. The industry's economics now depend on the products bettors understand least, parlays above all, on both sportsbooks and exchanges. That creates a durable opening for a tool whose only loyalty is to the user, at a moment when the heaviest users are losing trust in the category. The public backlash is a tailwind for that position and a headwind for nearly everything else on the list.

Three things should happen before much code is written. Spend a week confirming directly, in the App Store and in bettor communities, that no small app already does the price tag well. Put a rough version in front of twenty real bettors and see whether the dollar figure changes what they do. And form the LLC, since Apple's rules, affiliate registrations and liability all assume one. The dates to watch are the Supreme Court's handling of the prediction-market cases, the final CFTC event-contract rule, and whether Congress repeals the loss-deduction cap before the 2027 filing season.
