# Clutterkin — design on one page

*The game is called Clutterkin, and so are its creatures: one Clutterkin, many Clutterkin. To the clearance man they are clutter; to you they become kin. That is the whole story in the name.*

**The pitch.** You are house-sitting for your great-aunt. The cats sleep all day because they fight the house all night (that is Pawst Lives). You try something else: you talk to it. The odd socks, burnt toast and dust under the bed have woken up restless, here and all along the lane, and you befriend them and battle alongside them before the clearance van comes on Monday.

**The goal.** Calm all eight places on the lane before Monday, when Mr. Spick's clearance van takes away anything that will not sit still. It is said in the first minute, by great-aunt Maribel on the phone, and it is always on screen as one line: the next thing to do, with a gold arrow at the edge of the view when that thing is out of sight. Tap the line for the story so far and how many places are calm.

**The loop.** Roam the lane → walk into a place through its gate → walk up to whoever is restless there, in any order you like → each meeting is a three-against-three, turn-based fight → win experience, and bring home any restless Clutterkin you calmed → walk home to rest and change your team of three → go further. Every place has a boss, who will face you once everyone else there is calm; calming it opens the next place.

**The world.** One open neighbourhood, seen from above, several screens across and down, that you roam freely: tap or drag and you go there, round whatever is in the way, your team trailing behind and the view following. There are no menus between places and no change of screen: it is all one map. Home (great-aunt Maribel's house) is in the middle with a green below it, where friends who are not walking with you potter about. Streets run out to eight places, the eight rooms of Pawst Lives: The Garden Gate, Tin Can Alley, The Midnight Pantry, The Wash, The Dusty Stacks, Moonlit Rooftops, The Tick-Tock Attic, The Roaring Dark. A place is a corner of the same map with its own boundary and a way in. From outside you see only that its bushes are rustling. Step through the gate, rested, and everyone restless there comes out into the open: four groups and the boss, each holding the ground it stands on. Walk up to a group and the fight starts; the way you are sent to one group never brushes past another. Home is where you rest: that ends the visit, and whoever you calmed is restless again by the time you go back. You call on one place at a time: while you are out on a visit to one, everyone in the others keeps to their cover.

Places are not all the same shape, on purpose. The garden is open ground: its groups can be met in any order. **Tin Can Alley** is a lane one group wide, walled with bins and crates, down the east edge of the world: there is no going round anyone, so its groups are met in turn from the gate up, and a tap past someone walks you up to them. Whoever is restless there hides in bins, not bushes. A place that is not open yet has a barricade in its gateway, which comes down when the place before it is calm.

**How Clutterkin join.** Three ways were considered:

1. **Reach out (chosen).** Every restless Clutterkin is in a *Huff*, shown as pips. Once a round your house-sitter may *Reach out* to one: 2 pips off, 3 if an Clutterkin it has a soft spot for is standing on your side. Every hit your team lands on it puts a pip back. At no pips it settles, sits the fight out, and comes home if you win.
2. **Keepsakes.** Each kind drops a lost bit of itself (a button, a tuft); return enough and one follows you home.
3. **Errands.** Each kind wants one thing put right in its place (find its pair, clear its shelf).

Reach out wins because it happens inside the fight and costs something real: the house-sitter's one action that round (so no patching up), and turns spent not hitting a thing that is still hitting you. It has no dice, so it never feels like a slot machine; no item to stock, so nothing to sell later; and it makes team-building matter twice (see soft spots). Keepsakes is a grind in a menu. Errands is the richest but needs forty hand-written quests; it is the natural way to win over *bosses* later. One friend per kind: calming a kind you already have sends it off happy and gives your friend of that kind the experience instead. Bosses are too proud to reach.

**Heirlooms.** About one restless Clutterkin in sixty is an *Heirloom*: the same kind in rare colours, as if handed down. You can see it glint while it waits. Looks only: it fights the same, and is one pip prouder. Calm it and it keeps its colours; calm one of a kind you already have and your friend takes them on. Every Heirloom is recoloured from the ordinary sprite in code, so rare ones cost no new art.

*Looking for them.* Today you can look, but nothing helps you: step into a place and everyone there is out in the open, so a glint is seen before any fight; rest at home and step in again and they are all rolled afresh. The scrapbook marks which kinds' Heirlooms you have seen. Not there yet: any way to better the odds, a list of which you have kept, and Heirlooms for the three you start with (they are never met restless, so theirs cannot turn up). Whatever is added should be this game's own idea, not a borrowed one. The one that fits the story best: a place you have calmed feels safe, so its Heirlooms come out more often there.

**Elements.** Six, in one ring. Each beats the next (damage ×1.5) and is beaten by the one before (×0.7):

**Spark** singes **Fluff** · Fluff mops up **Suds** · Suds wash away **Crumb** · Crumb jams **Clank** · Clank mows **Sprout** · Sprout grounds Spark.

One element per Clutterkin and per move; no double types. An Clutterkin's *soft spot* is the element it beats: it lets its guard down around things it is not afraid of. So the friend that helps you calm it is the one it hits hardest.

**Stats and moves.** Four stats: **Health**, **Oomph** (hitting), **Grit** (taking hits), **Pep** (who goes first). Each grows 10% of its base per level. Damage = move power × Oomph × (Oomph ÷ Grit) × element × a ±10% wobble. No misses, no critical hits. Each Clutterkin learns three moves, at levels 1, 3 and 6, of five kinds: *hit* one, *sweep* all, *mend*, *boost* a stat, *sap* a stat. Strong moves need turns to recharge; nothing runs out. Anyone can *Brace* instead. Each round everyone standing acts once, fastest first; a boss acts twice. Once a round the house-sitter makes one *Call*: Reach out, Cheer on (+30% to a friend's next move) or Patch up (heal 20%, every other round).

**Getting stronger.** Health carries from one meeting to the next for as long as a visit lasts, wherever you walk, with a breather after each win (everyone regains 30%). Resting at home, or calming a place's boss, puts everyone back to full. Worn out, the team is carried home; nothing is lost but the walk, and whoever was knocked down first is still learned from. A won fight gives every friend on the team full experience and friends at home half. **Later:** at a set level an Clutterkin *grows up* into a new form (needs new art; the data hook is in place, the rule is not).

**The human cast.** People, not animals; the cats are a cameo, asleep on things.

- **The house-sitter** (you; named by the player). Up all night in pyjamas with a lamp. Patient rather than brave. Painted from the side, the front and behind, so they turn to face whichever way they walk.
- **Great-aunt Maribel**, whose house it is and who has always known. Away on a trip; she rings at the very start and sets the goal.
- **Ines Tock**, who mends clocks and has lived among Clutterkin all her life. She waits on the green below home and is the one who shows you how it is done: stand up for yourself if you must, Reach out if you can, and every place has a ringleader. You report back to her as each place is calmed.
- **Dov**, the kid who collects bottle caps. By the garden gate with a tip every time you ask (soft spots, Heirlooms, one of each kind, what to take down the alley).
- **Mr. Spick** of Spick & Span Clearances, who is paid to empty the lane on Monday and sees only junk. Not a villain so much as a deadline: he stands by the green counting how many places are still restless. THE VACUUM is his.

The three neighbours stand in the world; you walk up to one and talk. Someone with something new to say has a gold mark over their head. Later, cast members walk with you and each brings a different set of Calls.

**What is saved.** One versioned record on the device, nowhere else: the random seed, the house-sitter's name and look, every friend (kind, level, experience, Heirloom or not), the team of three, where you are standing in the world, progress in each place, the visit in progress (team Health, every group that is restless there and which of them are calm), which conversations have been had, which kinds have been met, a few counters and settings. In the iOS app the same record is also kept in the app's native storage, which iOS does not clear. A fight in progress is not saved; reloading puts you back in front of it, free to meet them or walk off. A save is rebuilt field by field when loaded, so an old, damaged or edited one cannot stop the game from starting.

**Not in the game.** Ads, analytics, accounts, network calls, purchases. Throwing things at monsters to catch them.
