# Clutterkin — design on one page

*The game is called Clutterkin, and so are its creatures: one Clutterkin, many Clutterkin. To the clearance man they are clutter; to you they become kin. That is the whole story in the name.*

**The pitch.** You are house-sitting for your great-aunt. The cats sleep all day because they fight the house all night (that is Pawst Lives). You try something else: you talk to it. The odd socks, burnt toast and dust under the bed have woken up restless, here and all along the lane, and you befriend them and battle alongside them before the clearance van comes on Monday.

**Two chapters.** The lane: eight places, to be calm before Monday. Then the far bank: four more, over the river. The first ends when the eighth place is calm and Ines Tock has been told; then the telephone rings by itself, and great-aunt Maribel's second call is the second chapter's beginning. Growth is meant to go on this way: one game that gets longer by whole chapters, each a new stretch of the same map with its own places, kinds and keys, rather than a new game each time. Chosen on 18 November 2026 ("Go with the four new places, I want the game to be longer than an hour").

**The goal.** Calm all eight places on the lane before Monday, when Mr. Spick's clearance van takes away anything that will not sit still. It is said in the first minute, by great-aunt Maribel on the phone, and it is always on screen as one line: the next thing to do, with a gold arrow at the edge of the view when that thing is out of sight. Tap the line for the story so far and how many places are calm.

**The loop.** Roam the lane → walk into a place through its gate or its front door → walk up to whoever is restless there, in any order you like → each meeting is a three-against-three, turn-based fight → win experience, and bring home any restless Clutterkin you calmed → walk home to rest and change your team of three → go further. Every place has a boss, who will face you once everyone else there is calm; calming it opens the next place.

**The world.** One open map, seen from above, three screens across and about seven down, that you roam freely: tap or drag and you go there, round whatever is in the way, your team trailing behind and the view following. It was one neighbourhood until 17 November 2026; the owner wanted it bigger, "with roads and sections between towns or areas". Now it is five stretches from top to bottom, and the heading names the one you are in. *The Lane* is the neighbourhood as it was: home (great-aunt Maribel's house) in the middle with a green below it, where friends who are not walking with you potter about; the garden and Tin Can Alley, corners of the same map walked straight into; and the Midnight Pantry. From its foot *Bramble Way* runs south through open country to *Lampwick Row* (the Wash and the Dusty Stacks); from the end of the Row a road crosses *Long Meadow* to *Chimney Hill* (Moonlit Rooftops and the Tick-Tock Attic); and at the bottom of Dark Lane, at the very end, is the far shed: the Roaring Dark. So the places come in the order you reach them walking, and the last is the furthest from home. The country between is hedgerows, haystacks, ponds, a well, and most of the quiet spots where strays turn up (see *Strays*); signposts say where each road goes. There is a bench on the Row and another on the Hill: sitting down rests everyone as home does, so nobody has to walk the whole way back to get their breath. Worn out anywhere, you are still carried all the way home. Ines Tock is still on the green, a long walk from the rows beyond, so there is a mailbox by home and by each of the four far houses: post her a note and her answer comes by return, word for word what she would have said to your face. (The first meeting is face to face.) And there is a map, opened from a *Map* button that sits beside the goal on the main screen (it was first only inside *What to do*, and the owner asked for it to be easier to find; it is still there too): the whole world on one small picture, drawn from the same lists the world is laid out from, with every place marked calm, open or still shut, the benches, where you are, and your goal. Eight places, the eight rooms of Pawst Lives: The Garden Gate, Tin Can Alley, The Midnight Pantry, The Wash, The Dusty Stacks, Moonlit Rooftops, The Tick-Tock Attic, The Roaring Dark. A place is a corner of the same map with its own boundary and a way in. From outside you see only that its bushes are rustling. Step through the gate, rested, and everyone restless there comes out into the open: four groups and the boss, each holding the ground it stands on. Walk up to a group and the fight starts; the way you are sent to one group never brushes past another. Home is where you rest: that ends the visit, and everyone is fresh. Whoever you have calmed stays calm, and only the others are waiting when you go back (see *Calm stays calm*). You call on one place at a time: while you are out on a visit to one, everyone in the others keeps to their cover.

The game fills whatever phone it is on. On a wider screen (a folding phone, open) you see more of the world rather than the same view blown up, lists go four to a row, and a fight's moves two to a row. Opening or closing the phone mid-game is just a change of size: nothing is lost.

Places are not all the same shape, on purpose. The garden is open ground: its groups can be met in any order. **Tin Can Alley** is a lane one group wide, walled with bins and crates, down the east edge of the world: there is no going round anyone, so its groups are met in turn from the gate up, and a tap past someone walks you up to them. Whoever is restless there hides in bins, not bushes. A place that is not open yet has a barricade in its gateway, which comes down when the place before it is calm.

**Indoors.** A building you can enter has a floor of its own. Tap its front, or use the button on its card: the screen dips to dark and you are on that floor, seen from the front (back wall above, floor below), walking about as you do outside; step onto the door mat and you are back in the lane at its door. *Home* is somewhere to be: a bed to rest in, the telephone (ring great-aunt Maribel and she reminds you what you were doing), the scrapbook on its shelf, the basket where the team is chosen, a fire, and whoever is not out walking with you. *The Midnight Pantry*, and the five places after it, are somewhere to calm: five groups spread about the floor, the ringleader at the far end, and everything about a visit as it is in the garden. Every one of the seven is painted (`docs/ART.md`): its back wall, its floor, and each thing that stands on it, which hides whoever walks behind it. Home is seen close up, so that a bed is a bed's length beside you; the places keep the lane's scale, so that a group is the size it is everywhere else. The lane is one area and each inside is another, joined by a door; a road to another town will be the same thing.

*Scale.* Out in the lane people are big for their buildings, as on any map. Indoors that will not do, so a room says how big people are drawn on its floor: at home nearly twice the lane's size, which makes the bookshelf a little taller than you and the bed a bed's length. A place where groups wait keeps the lane's scale, and its floor is drawn to that. One cheat is kept, as in any game of this kind: a room is bigger inside than its building looks from outside.

**The three you set out with.** Great-aunt Maribel's own: Hopmote (the dust under the spare bed), Eelastic (an odd sock) and Crumbwisp (a slice of burnt toast). The owner asked for the start of the game to say more about them, "to build some type of emotional attachment". So, once, straight after Maribel's first call, each has a page of its own: its picture, what it is, where it came from, and what it is like in a fight (which a test holds to the numbers). The game is about things that were left, lost or forgotten, and each of these was: Hopmote has minded what rolls under the bed for years and is frightened of one sound; Eelastic went into the wash as one of a pair and came out alone; Crumbwisp was forgotten in the toaster. Each story began in one place on the lane, and when that place is calm something is put right, said with the news that it is calm: in the Midnight Pantry Crumbwisp looks into the empty toaster; in the Wash Eelastic finds a sock behind Machine Six; and at the very end, in the Roaring Dark, Hopmote walks up to THE VACUUM on its own. Their stories are on their cards under Friends, and when you rest in your own bed Hopmote has slept on your feet. A save from before this was written is told about them once, as old friends. The last page first read "They are not pets, and they are not yours"; the owner did not like being told that, and wanted them shown as friends or companions. It now reads "From tonight they are your companions, and they would like to be your friends", and the pages are headed *Your three companions*. (They can be given names of your own: see *A name of your own*.)

**Strays.** Out on the lane itself, between places, a few small groups of lost Clutterkin wander: four to seven groups each time you set out rested, one, two or three in each, at quiet spots beside the streets. The owner asked for somewhere to "go in the wild" and level up. They are whoever lives in the places that are open to you, about as strong as whoever waits at the first two stops of the furthest of those, so they are worth meeting however far you have got and never spoil a place you have not reached. Nobody has to meet one: they hold no ground, the way found for you goes round them, and a fight starts only when you walk right up (or tap one). They are seen against a painting of the lane at night. Meeting them wears the team as any fight does, and that is kept even with no visit on: step into a place afterwards and you step in as you are; rest at home and everyone is fresh, and a different lot is out. Lose to them and you are carried home, as anywhere. They have dice of their own, so who is out on the lane never changes how a fight in a place falls out.

**Calm stays calm.** Whoever you calm stays calm. A place remembers which of its groups you have calmed, through resting at home and through being carried home worn out, so you never calm the same group twice to get at its ringleader: go home as often as you like, and when the other four are calm the ringleader faces you, however many outings that took. (The owner asked for this on 16 November 2026, having had to calm the garden's groups over again to reach its boss. Before then a rest made everyone in a place not yet calm restless again.) Calm the ringleader and the whole place is calm: its five groups and its ringleader do not come back, and the tick on its gate stays. Stepping into a calm place is a stroll. One to three small groups wander about some of its stops, different each time you come back from a rest; nobody has to be met. They are the place's own kinds at its own levels, so they are how you make the friends you missed, and where Heirlooms are looked for. The same goes, in a small way, for a place not yet calm: once two or more of its stops are calm, one to three groups wander about them each time you set out, always leaving one calm stop empty, marked *wandering* and never needing to be met. That is so nobody is ever stuck in front of a ringleader with no one to practise on: there is always somebody to grow stronger against, and a fight you lose still pays experience for everyone you knocked down. (Tin Can Alley is the exception: it is one group wide, so nobody wanders there until it is calm, and practice is a walk back to the garden.) What this costs is tension: a place can no longer be failed, only taken in more than one go. The simulator's "first visit" figures are now the share who do it in one go without going home.

**How Clutterkin join.** Three ways were considered:

1. **Reach out (chosen).** Every restless Clutterkin is in a *Huff*, shown as pips. Once a round your house-sitter may *Reach out* to one: 2 pips off, 3 if an Clutterkin it has a soft spot for is standing on your side. Every hit your team lands on it puts a pip back. At no pips it settles, sits the fight out, and comes home if you win.
2. **Keepsakes.** Each kind drops a lost bit of itself (a button, a tuft); return enough and one follows you home.
3. **Errands.** Each kind wants one thing put right in its place (find its pair, clear its shelf).

Reach out wins because it happens inside the fight and costs something real: the house-sitter's one action that round (so no patching up), and turns spent not hitting a thing that is still hitting you. It has no dice, so it never feels like a slot machine; no item to stock, so nothing to sell later; and it makes team-building matter twice (see soft spots). Keepsakes is a grind in a menu. Errands is the richest but needs forty hand-written quests; it is the natural way to win over *bosses* later. One friend per kind: calming a kind you already have sends it off happy and gives your friend of that kind the experience instead. Bosses are too proud to reach.

**Heirlooms.** About one restless Clutterkin in sixty is an *Heirloom*: the same kind in rare colours, as if handed down. You can see it glint while it waits. Looks only: it fights the same, and is one pip prouder. Calm it and it keeps its colours; calm one of a kind you already have and your friend takes them on. Every Heirloom is recoloured from the ordinary sprite in code, so rare ones cost no new art.

*Looking for them.* A small game of its own, for after a place is calm. Step into a place and whoever is there is out in the open, so an Heirloom is seen glinting before any fight (and heard, and a gold notice says so). A place you have calmed stays calm, and the few who wander about in it are different each time you come back from a rest. It feels safe to them, and they bring out their best colours there: one in twenty, not one in sixty. Word gets round, too: in a calm place kinds like the three you set out with come calling, which is the only way theirs can turn up. The scrapbook keeps the hunt: how many of the eighteen you have kept, which you have only seen, who is still to find, and where is calm. Dov will tell you where to look. Not there yet: any second way to better the odds. Whatever is added should be this game's own idea, not a borrowed one.

**Elements.** Six, in one ring. Each beats the next (damage ×1.5) and is beaten by the one before (×0.7):

**Spark** singes **Fluff** · Fluff mops up **Suds** · Suds wash away **Crumb** · Crumb jams **Clank** · Clank mows **Sprout** · Sprout grounds Spark.

One element per Clutterkin and per move; no double types. An Clutterkin's *soft spot* is the element it beats: it lets its guard down around things it is not afraid of. So the friend that helps you calm it is the one it hits hardest.

**Reading a fight.** A fight says what it is doing in plain words. Types and numbers are never left to be guessed (the owner, 18 November 2026: "I want their types to be obvious. And what oomph and grit are explained at first so you know what it means"): in a fight each fighter has its element written under it in that element's colour; out in the world, so has whoever is waiting, before you walk up; and on anyone's card each of the four numbers says what it is (Oomph: how hard it hits. Grit: how well it shrugs hits off). Before a save's first fight, four small pages explain it: the ring of elements, the four numbers, the pips and what Reach out does to them, and your own three Calls; they can be read again from the fight's menu. Choosing who to hit, the tag over each target says what the move will do to it, *Hurts more* or *Hurts less*, never a word that could be read the other way round. Reach out to one that is not ready and it turns its back, a bubble over its head says "Not yet!", the line below says how its kind says no ("Pricklebud bristles."), and then how many pips there are to go. Nobody dies: a friend whose Health runs out sits the fight out, and if all three do you carry them home.

**Walking into a fight.** A scuffle of dust billows up from where they stand until it fills the screen; the fight is set out behind it; and it clears from the middle to show them. A ringleader's is the colour of night and holds a moment longer. About a second, a plain fade for anyone who has asked their phone for less movement.

**Names.** Each kind has one coined name of its own, short enough for a fight's single line: Hopmote (the dust under the bed), Eelastic (an odd sock), Crumbwisp (burnt toast), and thirty-seven more; all forty are in `web/js/core/balance.js`. The ringleaders are Old Summerlong, Baron Pilfer, the Crumbwyrm, and the five whose names Pawst Lives gave them: the Spin Cycle, the Overdue Tome, the Night Warden, Grandfather Golem and THE VACUUM. Every kind's name was looked for in other games before use (wiki title lookups and full lists of card and creature names), and nine first choices were dropped for being taken or too close. The three chosen to replace the last of those (Dribblewax, Gutterflit, Embernip) were looked for on 17 November 2026 and nothing by those names was found. None of this is a trademark search, and one large wiki of creature names would not answer automated lookups, so it was covered only through a list of its names and through web search. The names of the forty grown-up forms were looked for on 18 November 2026, by a wider search of the same kind: card and creature databases, the page titles of some 225 game wikis, about a million titles for near-misses, two app stores, and US word marks through one aggregator. Thirty-one came back with nothing. Four were dropped for cause: Anacorda (a live US trademark, for charging cables of all things), Jackanapes (a monster in two well-known series of games), Lintelope (one letter from a creature in another creature-collecting game) and Grotesque (a monster's name in several games). Five more were replaced to be safe, or because the painting had moved on from the name: Gloomdrift, Appendix, Clampodile, Slatedrake and Candelabrute. The nine names that took their places are Hartmote, Drumcoil, Harlecrank, Grimspout, Shroudmote, Marginalia, Clipgator, Slateridge and Everwick. Seven of those went through the same search and came back clear, or with one weak near-miss (Hartmote is two letters from an obscure creature elsewhere and sounds nothing like it; Marginalia is an ordinary word and the title of somebody's game, but no creature's name). Two, Drumcoil and Shroudmote, were looked for only in three card and creature databases, where nothing was found. Three were kept with weak near-misses noted: Crustwraith, Wishmane and Clangster. Sources that would not answer are listed in the session's notes, not here. As before, none of this is a trademark search.

**A name of your own.** Since 18 November 2026 a friend can be given a name of your own: Friends, its card, *Give it a name*; up to twelve letters. It is then used wherever its kind's name was: on its card (which still says what kind it is), on the team, in a fight, in what a fight left behind, and in the story of the three you set out with ("Biscuit has lived under the spare bed..."). *Just Hopmote* takes it away again; the kind's name is never lost. The owner asked for "some type of emotional attachment to the game": the three have their stories, and this is the same thing for the other thirty-seven, in the player's own hands. A name is the second piece of text the save holds (the first is the house-sitter's own name). It is tidied when typed and again when a save is read: one line, no marks that draw nothing or turn the line round, cut to twelve letters; and it is only ever drawn as text. The rule is in `web/js/core/names.js`.

**Stats and moves.** Four stats: **Health**, **Oomph** (hitting), **Grit** (taking hits), **Pep** (who goes first). Each grows 10% of its base per level. Damage = move power × Oomph × (Oomph ÷ Grit) × element × a ±10% wobble. No misses, no critical hits. Each Clutterkin learns three moves, at levels 1, 3 and 6, of five kinds: *hit* one, *sweep* all, *mend*, *boost* a stat, *sap* a stat. Strong moves need turns to recharge; nothing runs out. Anyone can *Brace* instead. Each round everyone standing acts once, fastest first; a boss acts twice. Once a round the house-sitter makes one *Call*: Reach out, Cheer on (+30% to a friend's next move) or Patch up (heal 20%, every other round).

**Getting stronger.** Health carries from one meeting to the next for as long as a visit lasts, wherever you walk, with a breather after each win (everyone regains 30%). Resting at home, or calming a place's boss, puts everyone back to full. Worn out, the team is carried home; nothing is lost but the walk, and whoever was knocked down first is still learned from. A won fight gives every friend on the team full experience and friends at home half. **A fight teaches by its size.** Each of them is worth something by its level, so two teach twice what one does and three, three times; a ringleader is worth four. And one met on its own teaches only 60% of its worth (`LONE_SHARE`), because three against one is hardly a fight; only a stray is ever met alone. The owner asked for this on 18 November 2026 ("You shouldn't earn as much from fighting one stray as a bigger battle"), and in the same breath that there be "enough ways to earn xp so that you don't feel stuck". So strays now come in threes as well as alone and in pairs, which is more to learn from out on the roads than before, not less: one alone teaches 12 to 49 depending how far along you are, two or three together 51 to 205, and a group in a place 42 to 237. The ways to get stronger are: the groups in a place (once each); whoever is wandering in a place that is calm or partly calm (different every visit); the strays (different after every rest, and a rest is a bench away); a lost fight, which still pays for whoever was knocked down; and friends at home, who learn half of everything. A test holds that at every stage of the game the strays of one outing are worth about a level to a team facing that place's ringleader (0.85 to 1.4 of one). When a fight is lost, the news says where the practice is. Since nobody is fought twice on the way to a ringleader (see *Calm stays calm*), each fight teaches 30% more than it first did (`XP_RATE`): without that the team fell behind the lane and the last places were a wall.

**The bridge.** A river runs from one side of the world to the other below Chimney Hill, and nobody walks on water. The one way over is Kettle Bridge, and across its near end stands a gate with eight locks: one for each key of the lane. It is shut until all eight places there are calm, and then open for good. So the keys, which were only something to show for a place, turn out to be for something; and nobody wanders into chapter two by accident. Strays keep to the near bank until the gate is open; after that there are a couple more groups about, on both banks.

**Lively tonight.** The owner asked that people keep coming back. Chapters answer that over months; this answers it from one day to the next. Each real day one calm place is lively: more wander there (three or four groups, where a calm place has one to three) and each is in its rare colours three times in twenty, where a calm place has one in twenty. Which place goes by the date alone, in an order of its own that has nothing to do with the map, round every calm place before any comes again; so it is never the same two days running, and a kind you are hunting has its night. It gives, and takes nothing: there is no streak to break, nothing to miss, no notification, and the rules still keep no clock (whoever runs the game tells it the day; a test tells it none, and then nothing is lively and every figure in these notes stands). What it is not: a daily reward to collect. I chose against one because a reward for turning up is a chore the day you do not want to play, and this game has nothing to pay out in.

**Keepsakes.** Asked for on 18 November 2026: "a legendary or something similar type creature at the end of each chapter... a grail of sorts with it's own color variations". Ours is called a *Keepsake*: one creature there is only one of, to a chapter. It is not restless and lives in no place. Once every place of its chapter is calm it is out at a spot of its own every time you set out: Lullawren (a nightingale that is a music box, and the lullaby you have been hearing all along the lane) in the old orchard; Farewhale (a ship in a bottle that brought its own sea) on the river's far bank. It is met like a stray, alone, in a fight of its own, at the strength of its chapter's last ringleader. It takes eight pips of reaching out, where nobody else takes more than six; and it cannot be beaten into staying: knocked out, it slips away unhurt and is there again after a rest. A sensible stand-in player who only reaches out makes a friend of it every time, in about four rounds. So what makes it a grail is not a low chance of success, which would only be dice; it is that there is one, that it comes last, and its colours. It is in its rare colours about one meeting in twenty; and once it is your friend it is out again only about one outing in ten, and only in those colours, for whoever wants the whole of it. It does not grow up. A Keepsake is what it is.

**The rival.** Asked for on 18 November 2026: "a side quest, or a villain to the game that's our counterpart ... just to add more depth". Ours is Tansy Brisk, junior clearance officer of Spick & Span Clearances, Mr. Spick's firm: he counts what is restless and she collects it. She is the house-sitter's opposite number, not a wrongdoer: she thinks a mess is something to put in a box, you think it is somebody to make friends with, and the lane is not big enough for both ideas. She waits by the road at five points, each once a place further on is calm (the garden, the pantry, the Dusty Stacks, the Roaring Dark, the Tinker's Shed), and stays there until you have had it out with her: there is no deadline and nothing is lost by walking past. A bout is her three against yours, as worn as yours are; her three are a Preenager (a feather duster), a Slipsud (soap) and a Steamstoat (a travel iron), which is what a cleaning firm would issue, and three different elements, so no one friend of yours settles it. They were issued, not found: they are in no Huff, and Reach out has nobody to reach. That is on purpose. Everywhere else the game rewards kindness in a fight; here is one opponent it does not work on, because hers already have somebody. Her levels were set with the simulator so that a rested team that has come straight there wins about nine bouts in ten, the first a little oftener (see `RIVAL` in `core/balance.js`); a loss carries you home, as any does, and she waits. What she says changes over the five: they are "Units" with numbers; then one sits on her foot all night and she does not write it up; by the fourth they have names (Flick, Sliver, Crease) and the sheet is in the river; by the fifth she has handed in her boxes and is staying over the water to tell the ones there that they are not clutter. The save keeps one number for all of it. In a bout she is not on the field, and nor are you: you both stand in the strip at the bottom, at either end of your three Calls (the owner asked for that, having seen her drawn in the scene).

**Ringleaders as friends.** Asked for on 18 November 2026: "while I don't think you should be able to reach out and tame the boss, I think they're cool and would want them on my team", and "only one ring leader can be in your party at a time". So the rule that a ringleader is too proud to reach stands, everywhere and always; what changes is that a calm ringleader comes out of doors. From the moment its place is calm it stands on the grass beside it, and it has a wish. The wishes are the errands this page set aside at the start as "the natural way to win over bosses later", cut down to what the save already shows, so nothing new is kept and none can be undone: a friend of every kind in the garden (Old Summerlong), an Heirloom kept (Baron Pilfer), your own Crumbwisp grown up (the Crumbwyrm, who forgot it), an Eelastic and a Frayling, which are a pair of socks (the Spin Cycle), forty kinds met (the Overdue Tome), the whole lane calm (the Night Warden), five friends grown up (Grandfather Golem), Tansy Brisk beaten five times, who then signs it out of the depot (THE VACUUM), three Heirlooms kept (the Prize Marrow), a friend of every kind in the Deep End (Old Clawfoot), thirty friends (Captain Twostroke), and a Patchpaw, the bear with one eye, to mend (Madam Treadle). They are spread on purpose: some fall out of ordinary play, some send you back to a calm place for a kind you missed, and the Heirloom ones are the long game, which the lively place each day feeds. Its card says what is seen of it, the wish, and how far along you are; the scrapbook says the same on its page. Wish granted, it asks for one more bout, a friendly one: it alone, with two turns a round as ever, at the strength it had in its place or, if you have come a long way since, a level behind your strongest friend. The wish is meant to be the hard part: a team that has just calmed the place wins the bout at least eight times in ten. Win and it comes home at that level. **As a friend** it is one of the team: one turn a round, and 55% of the Health it had (about 66 before levels, where the sturdiest ordinary kind has 60 and a Keepsake 74 or more), with its own three moves, which hit no harder than anyone's. That is a little stronger than an ordinary friend, which is the owner's condition for the rule: only one ringleader walks with you at a time. The Friends screen offers a second ringleader only a swap with the first, and says why; the game refuses a team of two, and a save that claims one is given somebody else. A ringleader does not grow up and has no rare colours. A save keeps a ringleader friend only if its place is calm.

**Fluff.** The small dust bunny with the red bow who guides the cat in Pawst Lives, where it is "a dust bunny that got out of THE VACUUM". THE VACUUM is the last ringleader of our lane, so here Fluff turns up when the lane is calm, and sits beside each Keepsake to show where it is. The journal's "Also: Follow Fluff" says where to walk. Its picture is the one painted for Pawst Lives, used as it is; it is the only creature in the game that is nobody's to calm or to keep. (Its name is also the name of an element. It was Fluff first, in the other game, so it stays Fluff.)

**Keys.** Each of the twelve places has a key (the far bank's four hang on hooks of their own, which appear on the ring with the second call). What follows was written of the lane's eight. Each of the eight places has a key, and its ringleader hands it over when the place is calm: the Gate Key from Old Summerlong (green iron, a leaf for a handle), the Tin Key from Baron Pilfer (cut from a can, a bottle cap for a handle), and so on to the Dark Key, which THE VACUUM swallowed years ago and gives back. Each is its own painting. The news that a place is calm shows its key and a line about it; the key ring is in *What to do* and in the scrapbook, eight in a row, the ones not yet earned as dark outlines of themselves, and a tap says what each is or where it is to be had. A key is not spent on anything and nothing needs one: it is what you have to show for a place, and a house-sitter is somebody trusted with the keys. You hold a place's key for exactly as long as the place is calm, which is for good, so the save keeps nothing more for them. The idea is the owner's (18 November 2026): other games give a token for beating each place's leader, "what if we got special looking keys when clearing an area or a house?" What keeps it this game's own: they are keys, because of who you are in this story; they hang on a ring, not in a case; nobody is beaten for one, it is handed over by somebody you have calmed; and they are never called anything but keys (a test holds that).

**Growing up.** A friend grows up. When it gains a level that takes it to its kind's level, or past it, it grows into its kind's grown form, for good: a new picture, drawn plainly bigger wherever it stands (in a fight, walking behind you, on Friends), its kind's new name (a Hopmote is a Hartmote; a name of your own is kept), and every stat 5% stronger. The three you set out with grow at level 8, about the time of the Midnight Pantry. Every other kind grows a few levels above the level it is first met at: 9 for a kind from the garden, up to 16 for one from the Roaring Dark. So growing up is earned at your side, and the kinds from far along are something to work toward after the lane is quiet. One that comes home already past its level grows at its next. The moment it happens is the first news after the fight: what it was and what it is now side by side, the new one twice the size, its new name, a sound of its own, and for each of the three a few words (Hopmote no longer fits under the spare bed, and the antlers are coat hangers from the wardrobe). A grown friend's card is tagged *Grown up* and says what it was when it was small; a young one's card says at what level it will grow, and not into what. The scrapbook counts how many kinds you have seen grown.

**The restless grow up too.** As the game advances, more of whoever is restless has grown up: none in the garden, Tin Can Alley or the Midnight Pantry; one in five in the Wash, rising a tenth a place to three in five in the Roaring Dark; and among the strays, as many as in the furthest place open to you. So the further you go, the more of a mix a fight is. A grown one stands bigger, goes by its grown name, is 5% stronger as a grown friend is, teaches a quarter more, and if you reach out to it and win it comes home grown, whatever its level. Ringleaders are as they are. (No dice are rolled for this where nobody can be grown, so the first three places fall out exactly as they did before.)

*How it came to be, 18 November 2026.* Growing up was at first left out of this version, for three reasons given at the time: forty paintings, new names for companions the opening had just introduced, and that of everything planned it is the idea nearest to another game's best-known mechanic. The owner read that and asked for it ("yes you can start on the new forms"), which settles it. What keeps it this game's own: what a household thing grows into is more of the same household thing (an odd sock into a stocking, a tin can into a dustbin, a candle into a giant of melted wax); it is called growing up and nothing else; nothing is used on a friend to make it grow, and it cannot be chosen or put off. The owner then said "Make sure the change is obvious enough". A first set of paintings showed the young one in the same pose wearing something new, and was set aside; the paintings in the game were briefed as new drawings: a new pose, a bigger build, a changed outline (the owner, on seeing the first of them: "the new images look amazing"). And then: "As the game advances, I also want you to fight the grown up friends too, a mix, not just the first stage", which is the paragraph above. The strength was set with the simulator. At 15% stronger its stand-in player could not lose anywhere past the alley, because the whole team grows about half-way through. At 5%, with the grown among the restless, each place from the Midnight Pantry to the Tick-Tock Attic is calmed in one go about 80 to 85 times in 100 (it was 55 to 65), and the Roaring Dark about half the time (it was about one in five), nine in ten by the second outing. That is easier than it was, which is the direction the owner asked for ("so that you don't feel stuck"), without making the end a formality. What makes growing up plain to see is the new form and its size, not the number.

**The human cast.** People, not animals; the cats are a cameo, asleep on things.

- **The house-sitter** (you; named by the player). Up all night in pyjamas with a lamp. Patient rather than brave. Painted from the side, the front and behind, so they turn to face whichever way they walk.
- **Great-aunt Maribel**, whose house it is and who has always known. Away on a trip; she rings at the very start and sets the goal.
- **Ines Tock**, who mends clocks and has lived among Clutterkin all her life. She waits on the green below home and is the one who shows you how it is done: stand up for yourself if you must, Reach out if you can, and every place has a ringleader. You report back to her as each place is calmed.
- **Dov**, the kid who collects bottle caps. By the garden gate with a tip every time you ask (soft spots, Heirlooms, one of each kind, what to take down the alley).
- **Mr. Spick** of Spick & Span Clearances, who is paid to empty the lane on Monday and sees only junk. Not a villain so much as a deadline: he stands by the green counting how many places are still restless. THE VACUUM is his.

The three neighbours stand in the world; you walk up to one and talk. Someone with something new to say has a gold mark over their head. Later, cast members walk with you and each brings a different set of Calls.

**The loading screen.** While the game's pictures load you are looking at the game itself: a postcard of the lane, with home in the middle and its windows lit, the house-sitter and their three at the door, the lamps, the stepping-stone paths, the neighbours, and the Moonlit Rooftops house below. It is the real world, painted by the game's own painters from the game's own art (`tools/postcard.mjs`), so it costs no new picture and stays true as the lane grows. It drifts very slowly toward the cottage while fireflies wink over it; the name is above, and below it says what is being got ready ("Finding the odd socks…") over a bar of lantern gold. The iPhone app's launch image is the same picture shown the same way, so the one becomes the other without a jump. It stays up for at least a second or so, since on a phone the loading itself is over almost at once.

**Sound.** Every sound is made in code as it is needed: there are no sound files, and nothing is fetched. Out in the lane a slow lullaby in three plays, as if from a music box; a fight has a quicker plucked tune in four; fights in Tin Can Alley have their own, a riff played on tin; and a ringleader has its own wherever it is met, lower and heavier. About thirty small effects say what is happening: footsteps (duller on grass, sharper on cobbles), the phone ringing, a voice of their own for each neighbour, the gate, a hit that really landed or barely did, a Huff pip coming off, a friend settling, a glint when an Heirloom is about. Sound effects and music each have a switch in Settings, and both are on to begin with. Nothing sounds before the first touch (a browser's rule), and on an iPhone the ring/silent switch silences it.

**What is saved.** One versioned record on the device, nowhere else: the random seed, the house-sitter's name and look, every friend (kind, level, experience, Heirloom or not), the team of three, where you are standing in the world (and, indoors, where on that floor), progress in each place, the visit in progress (team Health, every group that is restless or wandering there and which of them are calm), which conversations have been had, which kinds have been met, a few counters and settings (fast fights, sound effects, music). In the iOS app the same record is also kept in the app's native storage, which iOS does not clear. A fight in progress is not saved; reloading puts you back in front of it, free to meet them or walk off. A save is rebuilt field by field when loaded, so an old, damaged or edited one cannot stop the game from starting.

**Not in the game.** Ads, analytics, accounts, network calls, purchases. Throwing things at monsters to catch them.
