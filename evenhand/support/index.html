Evenhand support

Evenhand is a private journal for separated parents. Everything stays on your phone.

What Evenhand is, and is not

Evenhand helps you keep a dated record, in your own words, of exchanges, parenting time, costs and what was said, and turns that record into a report. It is a record-keeping tool. It is not a law firm, it does not give legal advice, and it cannot tell you what a court will decide. Whether and how a court uses any record is for the court; ask an attorney licensed in your state how to present yours.

Writing entries

Tap the round + button and choose what you are recording: an exchange, an incident, a communication, an expense, a payment, a child event or a note. Write what you saw and heard, when, and where. If Wording hints is on (under More), the app suggests plainer wording when an entry uses words like "always" or guesses at someone's reasons. The hints never change what you write.

Sealing

For 15 minutes after you first save an entry you can edit it or delete it. Then it seals: the app no longer lets it be edited or deleted. You can seal an entry sooner from its page. Each sealed entry gets a permanent number and a fingerprint worked out from its contents and from the entry before it.

Correcting a sealed entry

Open the entry and tap Add an amendment. The original stays as it was; your correction is added beneath it with the date you wrote it. If an entry should not be there at all, Withdraw this entry marks it as withdrawn. A withdrawn entry stays in your record and is left out of reports unless you choose to include withdrawn entries.

Photos

Add photos, receipts and screenshots to an entry while you write it. The app keeps its own resized copy, without the location details a photo can carry. The free version holds 12 photos; Evenhand Plus has no limit.

Parenting time

The calendar records where the children spent each night. Tap a day and choose With you, with the other parent, or Somewhere else. A night you have not recorded is shown as not recorded: the app never assumes it went as planned.

If you add your schedule (More, then Schedule), the calendar shows what was planned with a dashed outline and marks in red any recorded night that differs. Record past nights as scheduled fills in the unrecorded past nights of the month you are looking at; use it only if those nights did go as scheduled.

The percentage shown is your share of the recorded nights the children spent with a parent. It is a count of what you recorded, not a legal figure.

Your state's law

When you choose your state, Evenhand shows the factors that state's custody statute lists for a judge to weigh, with the citation and a link to the text. You can file an entry under one or more factors, and a report can group your entries the same way. The titles and summaries are plain-English wording kept close to the statute. They are legal information, not legal advice.

Some states' statutes list no factors. For a few of those the app uses the list from a court decision that the state's courts follow, and says so; for the rest it offers general topics. Where the current text of a state's list has not yet been confirmed against the statute, the app says so and offers general topics rather than risk showing something out of date. Laws change: the link on that screen always shows the current wording.

Reports

Under Reports, choose a period. The summary on screen, Export as text and Export as CSV are free. Make PDF report is part of Evenhand Plus: a dated, page-numbered document with a summary, your entries in the order things happened, nights by month, an expense ledger, your entries grouped by factor, and each photo as a numbered exhibit with an index.

A report includes sealed entries only. If some entries in the period are not yet sealed, the app asks before sealing them.

Keeping your journal private

More, then App lock, sets a passcode for Evenhand that is separate from your phone's. When you set it, the app shows a recovery code once. Write it down and keep it away from your phone: if you forget the passcode, the recovery code is the only other way in.

The journal is hidden in the app switcher. Reminders use neutral wording unless you turn Discreet wording off.

Backing up

Your journal is on your phone only. If you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, your journal is part of that backup. For a copy you hold yourself, More, then Backup and export, makes a backup file encrypted with a passphrase you choose. Save it to Files, a computer or a drive you trust. To restore, choose the file on the same screen and enter its passphrase; restoring replaces what is in the app on that phone.

There is no way to recover a backup without its passphrase, and no copy of your journal anywhere we could recover it from.

Checking the record

More, then Record integrity, re-checks every fingerprint. Each report prints the fingerprint the whole record had when the report was made, so a later copy of the record can be checked against it. Dates and times come from your phone's clock, so the fingerprints do not prove on their own when an entry was written.

Evenhand Plus

Plus adds PDF reports and unlimited photos. It is offered yearly (with a free trial for new subscribers), monthly, or as a single lifetime purchase; the app shows the prices for your country before you buy. Writing and sealing entries, the calendar, the app lock, text and CSV export and backups are free and stay free. If a subscription ends, everything you wrote is still there and can still be read and exported.

To cancel or change a subscription: iPhone Settings, your name, Subscriptions, or More, Evenhand Plus, Manage subscription in the app. To get Plus back on a new phone, tap Restore purchases. Refunds are handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

If you are not safe

If you or your children are in immediate danger, call 911. In the United States, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free and open at every hour: call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788.
