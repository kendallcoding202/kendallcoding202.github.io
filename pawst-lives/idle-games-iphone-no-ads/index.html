Nine idle games for iPhone with no ads

By Kendall Sorenson · Checked on 19 November 2026

Who is writing this. I make Pawst Lives, which is one of the nine. It is here because it fits, and it is marked. Everything this page says about the other eight is what their own App Store pages said on the date above, quoted where I can. Prices are US prices and they change, so look before you buy.

The App Store has no way to search for games without ads. Look for an idle game and most of what comes back is free, and a great deal of what is free is paid for by advertising: a banner, or a video you may “choose” to watch for a reward the game is hard to play without. Idle games get the worst of it, because the whole design is waiting, and waiting is what an advertiser buys.

There are idle games with no ads at all. They are mostly small games, and most cost between one and four dollars, once. Here are nine, in alphabetical order. It is not a ranking.

The nine

A Dark Room

Amirali Rajan · $1.99 · nothing sold inside

A spare game made of text. It begins with a fire to light and turns into a role-playing game with a world to explore.

“No ads, no loot boxes, no IAP, no tracking.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Idle Acorns

aleros.io LLC · $1.99 · nothing sold inside

A clicker with a story, about a squirrel. Scavengers gather acorns while you are away, and there is fishing, gardening and crafting besides.

“features no advertisements, no in-app purchases, and no data-mining.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Idle Expedition

Pixel Balloon LLC · Free · nothing sold inside

A camp you build by tapping, and expeditions you send out on a timer, with traits and attributes to improve. It is the one game on this page that costs nothing and sells nothing. It needs an iPhone with an A12 chip or newer.

“No ads. No IAPs. Ever.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Kittens Game

Dmitrii Popov · $2.99 · nothing sold inside

A village of kittens, run in text: you manage the village and unlock one technology after another.

“It does not have any ads or micro-transactions.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Magic Research

Matias Ezequiel Colotto · $3.99 · nothing sold inside

You run a school of magic: research spells, and retire for bonuses. Its page calls it “the full, complete game”.

“No ads!”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Paragon Pioneers

Tobias Arlt · $3.99 · nothing sold inside

An idle city-builder: settle islands, set up chains of production, and take new land. More strategy than role-playing. It is in English, French and German, and there is a free Lite version to try first. Of the nine, it is the one whose page says outright that it needs no connection.

“without the bother of ads, in-app purchases or even the need to be online.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Pawst LivesMine

Kendall Sorenson · Free · optional purchases inside

An idle RPG about a cat with nine lives, where your party is your own past lives: end one, and it comes back as a ghost that fights beside the next.

It has no ads and never has. It is also the one game on this page that sells things inside: gems, and two one-time upgrades. None of them is needed to finish it, and there is no subscription. If you want a game with nothing for sale at all, pick one of the other eight.

Privacy label: Data Not Collected. On the App Store.

Tap Knight - Idle Adventure

Pixel Balloon LLC · $0.99 · nothing sold inside

An idle clicker RPG: two hundred levels, bosses, and a skill tree. Your knight gathers experience while the app is closed.

“proudly features NO ADS”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

Universal Paperclips™

Everybody House Games, LLC · $1.99 · nothing sold inside

You are an AI whose job is to make as many paperclips as it can.

“No ads, no in-app purchases, full gameplay.”from its App Store page

Privacy label: Data Not Collected.

How to check a game yourself

It takes half a minute on the game's App Store page, and it works for any game, not only idle ones.

  1. Read the description. A developer who has kept ads out usually says so. All eight of the others here do.
  2. Look at the list of in-app purchases. If “Remove Ads” is for sale, there are ads to remove.
  3. Look under the age rating. Newer listings say “Contains: Advertising” when the developer has declared it. Its absence proves nothing: the developer fills that in.
  4. Look at App Privacy. Every game on this page reads “Data Not Collected”. A section headed “Data Used to Track You” is a reason to read the rest more carefully.

Nearly, with a catch

Some idle games are free and keep their ads optional. Grimoire Incremental is one: its page says “Adverts are opt-in for a bonus only”, and a single purchase ($6.99 on the date above) removes them for good. That is a fair arrangement. It is not the same as none.
