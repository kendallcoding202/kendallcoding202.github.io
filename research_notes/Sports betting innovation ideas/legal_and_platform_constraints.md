## 1. What makes something illegal gambling in the US, and how do the main workarounds function?

### Takeaway
Gambling = prize + chance + consideration; every "workaround" removes or weakens one element (free entry removes consideration, skill contests attack chance, fantasy sports has a federal UIGEA carve-out plus state statutes, CFTC event contracts rely on federal preemption). In 2025-2026 nearly every workaround except truly free-to-play and plain content/tools came under active state attack, so none of them is "safe by structure" any more.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's own rule restates the three-element test: "Lottery apps must have consideration, chance, and a prize." [F] — [Apple App Review Guidelines 5.3.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- UIGEA defines a "bet or wager" as "the staking or risking by any person of something of value upon the outcome of a contest of others," a sporting event, or a game subject to chance, and also covers "the purchase of a chance or opportunity to win a lottery or other prize." [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- UIGEA is not itself a legalization statute: "unlawful internet gambling" means a bet that "is unlawful under any applicable Federal or State law" where initiated, received or made, so state law controls legality. [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362(10)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- **Free-to-play carve-out (federal):** UIGEA excludes "participation in any game or contest in which participants do not stake or risk anything of value other than" personal effort or "points or credits that the sponsor of the game or contest provides to participants free of charge." [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362(1)(E)(viii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- **Fantasy sports carve-out (federal):** excluded only if (1) prizes are "established and made known to the participants in advance," (2) outcomes "reflect the relative knowledge and skill of the participants" and are "determined predominantly by accumulated statistical results of the performance of individuals," and (3) no winning outcome is based on "the score, point-spread, or any performance or performances of any single real-world team" or "solely on any single performance of an individual athlete in any single real-world sporting or other event." [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362(1)(E)(ix)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- **CFTC carve-out (federal):** UIGEA excludes "any transaction conducted on or subject to the rules of a registered entity or exempt board of trade" under the Commodity Exchange Act. [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362(1)(E)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- **Skill contests — three state tests:** the "dominant factor" (predominance) test is the most common; the "material element" test disqualifies a game if chance plays a significant role even when skill dominates; the "any chance" test "can render wagering on most any game illegal gambling." [S] — [Walters Law Group skill-gaming guide](https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-legal-guide/)
- Paid-entry skill contests are not uniformly legal: in South Carolina even skill games with entry fees are prohibited; real-money skill sites typically exclude Louisiana; Florida prohibits wagering on skill games by statute; Delaware and Washington are also described as strict. [S] — [Walters Law Group state list](https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-states/); enforcement trend against skill games in "outlier states" discussed (March 2026) at [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/) [S]
- **State DFS law is unsettled even where there is a federal carve-out:** California AG Rob Bonta issued a formal opinion (July 2025) concluding daily fantasy sports contests are illegal under state law; the opinion is advisory and no cease-and-desists followed, but PrizePicks and Underdog switched California pick'em to peer-to-peer formats. [S] — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/prizepicks-switches-to-peer-to-peer-games-in-california-ahead-of-ag-ruling-july-3-2025); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/237843/underdog-dfs-goes-p2p-in-california-amid-legal-uncertainty/)
- "Against-the-house" pick'em is the most attacked DFS format: one 2026 tracker lists Arizona, Colorado, Idaho, Iowa, Louisiana, Montana, Nevada and Washington as banning pick'em DFS (sources conflict on individual states); Underdog pick'em entries stopped in New York as of March 2025; Alabama's AG forced product changes in June 2026. [S] — [DeucesCracked DFS regulation 2026](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/dfs-daily-fantasy-regulation-2026); [Legal Sports Report DFS legal states](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/dfs-sites/legal-states/)
- **Social gambling exemptions:** most states that allow home poker/office pools do so via a narrow social-gambling carve-out, typically conditioned on no house take, a private setting and no profit to the host; a law-firm 50-state survey notes several states where social gambling is not clearly defined; counts of "legal" states vary by source (41 to 45+). Florida caps winnings at $10 per game. [S] — [FordHarrison 50-State Survey on Social Gambling Laws](https://www.fordharrison.com/files/28045_50%20State%20Survey%20on%20Gambling%20Laws%20March%202014.pdf) (2014-2015, dated)
- A P2P sports-betting operator (BettorEdge) claims its real-money product is legal in "approximately 90% of US states" — promotional, unverified. [S] — [BettorEdge help center](https://bettoredge.tawk.help/article/is-bettoredge-legal-in-my-state)
- Peer-to-peer real-money sports exchanges have been migrating to CFTC registration rather than relying on social-gambling or sweepstakes theories: ProphetX received DCM/DCO approval June 11, 2026; Novig launched nationally Aug 4, 2026 via the CFTC-designated Ludlow Exchange (and immediately sued New York). [F] — [RotoWire prediction markets legal timeline](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- **Sweepstakes dual-currency model mechanics:** "Gold Coins" are purchased for play and are not redeemable; "Sweeps Coins" are redeemable for cash and given "free" with Gold Coin purchases (consideration is argued away via free alternative entry). California AB 831 now defines exactly this structure, when it simulates gambling (including sports wagering) and awards cash, as illegal. [F] — [ZwillGen on AB 831](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- Legitimate promotional sweepstakes tied to bona fide product sales remain lawful even under AB 831. [F] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- **CFTC event contracts:** whether sports event contracts are "swaps" under exclusive CFTC jurisdiction is the subject of a live circuit split (3rd Cir. yes; 9th and 6th Cir. no) — see Section 4. [F] — [RotoWire timeline](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)

### Inferences
- The only structurally robust workaround for an unlicensed solo dev is removing consideration entirely (free entry, sponsor-funded prizes) — it is the one exclusion that is explicit in federal law and not contested by states. Even then Apple may treat it as gambling (Section 6).
- "Skill" framing is state-by-state and fact-specific; a sports-prediction contest with an entry fee looks far more like the excluded "single team / single performance" conduct than like season-long fantasy, so it would not fit the UIGEA fantasy carve-out unless built as a multi-athlete roster/statistics game.
- A social-gambling/P2P app in which the developer takes a rake or fee generally breaks the "no house profit" condition most social exemptions rely on; the fact that funded P2P startups (Novig, ProphetX) moved to CFTC registration suggests the unlicensed P2P route was not considered defensible at scale.
- Lawyer review is required for any product with (a) an entry fee, (b) a cash-redeemable currency, or (c) user-to-user money movement.

### Gaps
- I did not open a primary 50-state table of gambling tests (dominant factor vs. material element vs. any chance); state assignments above come from search summaries of law-firm guides (some dated 2018-2023).
- I could not verify a current list of states with explicit social-gambling exemptions; the only survey found is from 2014-2015.
- Federal statutes other than UIGEA (Wire Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1955 Illegal Gambling Business Act, state money-transmitter laws for holding user stakes) were not researched in this session; from background knowledge they are relevant to anyone holding or transmitting wagers, but no source was captured.

## 2. What does it cost to become a licensed sportsbook or DFS operator, and why is this out of reach for a solo dev?

### Takeaway
Sportsbook licensing is a per-state, six-to-eight-figure exercise (application/licence fees from ~$50k to $25M per state before taxes, bonding, tech certification, and usually a market-access deal with a casino/tribe/team); DFS licensing is cheaper (e.g., $15k initial fee in Kentucky) but is also per-state, increasingly taxed, and legally contested in major states. Neither is realistic for a solo developer.

### Cited Findings
- North Carolina: operator licence application fee $1,000,000; service provider licence $50,000; supplier licence $30,000. [S] — [NC State Lottery Commission / ncgaming.gov](https://ncgaming.gov/?p=8424)
- New York (capped at nine operators) has the highest initial fee at $25 million. [S] — [iGaming Business US wagering tax rates](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/online-sports-betting/wagering-tax-rates-us/); a vendor blog instead lists $2M (conflict; the $25M figure is the more credible) [S] — [Track360 state-by-state map](https://track360.io/blog/us-sports-betting-state-by-state-operator-map-2026)
- Arizona: event wagering operator licence — $100,000 application fee, $750,000 initial licence fee (five years), $150,000 annual fee; a "management service provider" licence is $1,000 application + $10,000 initial licence. [S] — [JD Supra summary of WA/AZ/WY sports betting](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/summary-of-sports-betting-in-washington-8737280)
- Tennessee: $50,000 non-refundable operator application fee, credited against the licensing fee on approval. [S] — [Tennessee Sports Wagering Council fee page](https://www.tn.gov/swac/forms/operator/fee.html)
- Ohio: $150,000 one-time application fee for sports gaming proprietors and $150,000 for mobile management services providers. [S] — [Ohio LSC 2024 state agency fees](https://www.lsc.ohio.gov/assets/organizations/legislative-service-commission/files/2024-state-agency-fees-cac.pdf)
- Industry characterisation: costs "range from 5-figure vendor permit fees to intensive 7-figure flagship platform operator license costs"; high costs have pushed funded operators out of states (Betr and WynnBet left Massachusetts in 2024). [S] — [iGamingFuture interview with BettingUSA CEO](https://igamingfuture.com/talking-the-licensing-process-in-the-us/); [Track360](https://track360.io/blog/us-sports-betting-state-by-state-operator-map-2026)
- A current state-by-state comparison exists in Foley & Lardner's 2026 Online Sports Betting Tracker (not opened). [S] — [Foley tracker PDF](https://www.foley.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Online-Sports-Betting-Tracker-26-6800.pdf)
- No national licence exists; licensing, taxes on GGR and market-access payments are per-state. [S] — [gamblingmaps.org operator regulations](https://gamblingmaps.org/map/regulations/united-states/for-operators)
- DFS — Kentucky: initial licence fee $15,000 for fantasy contest providers not already holding a sports wagering licence (statute effective July 15, 2026). [S] — [Kentucky Revised Statutes](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/Statutes/statute.aspx?id=58098)
- DFS — Illinois enacted its first DFS framework in the FY2027 budget, effective July 1, 2026: 15% privilege tax on adjusted gross fantasy contest receipts and two-tier licensing based on prior-year user count; operators had until September 2026 to comply. [S] — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/illinois-first-ever-dfs-framework-takes-effect-operators-have-until-sept-to-comply); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/illinois-enacts-first-ever-fantasy-sports-regulatory-framework)
- DFS — Connecticut (2021) required applicants to already hold a valid licence in another state and to pay for past operations. [S] — [CT Governor press release](https://portal.ct.gov/governor/News/Press-Releases/2021/07-2021/Governor-Lamont-Announces-Provisional-Licenses-Issued-for-Daily-Fantasy-Sports)
- Platform rules assume a licensed legal entity: Apple says gambling apps "should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer" [F] — [Apple Guidelines 5.1.1(ix)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); Google Play requires "a valid operating license for each country or state/territory" and each product type [F] — [Google Play real-money gambling policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Even the CFTC route used by new entrants requires heavyweight registration: DCM applications are reviewed against 23 core principles with a 180-day review clock that only starts when the application is "materially complete"; FCMs must meet net-capital rules and segregate customer funds. [F] — [WilmerHale, Apr 15, 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260415-want-to-get-into-cftc-regulated-event-contract-markets-heres-how-it-works)

### Inferences
- Even a single-state sportsbook launch implies several hundred thousand dollars in non-refundable fees plus background investigations of principals, certified platform/geolocation/KYC vendors and ongoing tax and compliance staff; a multi-state footprint is tens of millions. This is why solo developers are confined to "adjacent" products.
- DFS is the cheapest licensed real-money category but requires dozens of state filings, state-specific contest restrictions, and is exposed to AG opinions (California) and new taxes (Illinois 15%); a solo dev could not realistically maintain it.

### Gaps
- Market-access (skin) deal pricing, surety bond amounts and typical licensing timelines per state were not found in sources opened this session. From background knowledge, many states require mobile operators to be tethered to a casino, tribe or pro team, but I captured no citation.
- The Foley 2026 tracker and state regulator fee schedules were not opened; fee figures above are from search summaries and should be re-verified before publication.
- No consolidated 2026 table of DFS licence fees was found.

## 3. Sweepstakes (dual-currency) model: state of the law 2025-2026 and viability for a new entrant

### Takeaway
The dual-currency sweepstakes model is in rapid legislative and enforcement retreat: roughly 9-12 states have statutory bans (including California and New York), more act through AG/regulator cease-and-desists and lawsuits, and new laws reach vendors — payment processors, geolocation providers, content suppliers and media affiliates. For a new solo entrant it is a high-risk path, and "sweepstakes sportsbooks" are explicitly covered by several bans.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory bans listed by Gambling.com: California, Connecticut, Indiana, Louisiana, Maine, Montana, New Jersey, New York and Tennessee, with Oklahoma's ban starting November 1, 2026. Trackers disagree on totals (six to twelve "bans or equivalent prohibitions" as of Aug 2026, with roughly fifteen more restricting or moving toward a ban). [S] — [Gambling.com sweepstakes legal states](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal); [DeucesCracked tracker](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/sweepstakes-casino-bans-lawsuits-2026-state-map); [Source Code Lab "17+ states"](https://sourcecodelab.co/news/sweepstakes-casino-crackdown-2026-states/)
- Per-state bill/effective dates as reported by one tracker (May 2026): California AB 831 (Jan 1, 2026); New York AB 6745 (Dec 5, 2025; subject to an SPGA legal challenge); New Jersey A 5447 (Aug 15, 2025); Montana SB 555 (May 12, 2025); Connecticut SB 1235 (Jun 11, 2025); Indiana HB 1052 (Jul 1, 2026); Maine LD 2007 (early July 2026); Washington treated as an existing prohibition. [F] — [DeucesCracked state status](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/sweepstakes-casino-bans-2026-state-status)
- Indiana HB 1052 was signed March 12, 2026 (effective July 1, 2026); Maine's ban took effect July 14, 2026; Tennessee's ban is listed as effective May 22, 2026. [S] — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal); [Gambling Insider May 2026 update](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/162789/sweepstakes-casino-monthly-update-may-2026-tennessee-louisiana-oklahoma-iowa)
- Oklahoma: lawmakers overrode the governor's veto to ban "online casino games" involving a "representative of value," explicitly covering dual-currency systems (SB 1589). [S] — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal); [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/sweepstakes-casino-bans-2026-state-status)
- **California AB 831** (signed Oct 11, 2025; effective Jan 1, 2026): prohibits operating or supporting an online sweepstakes game that uses dual currency, simulates gambling (slots, video poker, table games, bingo **and sports wagering**) and awards cash or equivalents; misdemeanour with up to one year in jail and up to $25,000 per violation; liability extends to payment processors, geolocation providers, gaming content suppliers and media affiliates who "knowingly and willfully" aid operation or promotion. [F] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- Connecticut SB 1235 expanded the state's sweepstakes ban to cover online simulated casino games **and sports wagering**. [S] — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/241681/california-sweepstakes-ban-bill-passes-legislature/)
- Indiana's 2026 law is reported to ban sweepstakes casinos **and sportsbooks** but to exclude peer-to-peer poker. [S] — [secondary poker news site](https://poker-rift.pages.dev/news/indiana-sweepstakes-poker-survives-gambling-ban-peer-to-peer-exemption/) (low-authority; verify against statute)
- Enforcement without new statutes: Illinois Gaming Board with the AG issued 60+ (reported as 65) cease-and-desist letters to online casino/sweepstakes platforms in 2026, reportedly including Chumba, LuckyLand and social sportsbook **Fliff**. [S] — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/109652/illinois-sweepstakes-casinos-cease-desist-letters); [Illinois Gaming Board release](https://www.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/illinois/iisnewsattachments/32173-020526-igb-r-cease-and-desist-online-casinos-final-25025.pdf.pdf); [Bettors Insider](https://bettorsinsider.com/casino/2026/05/12/illinois-gaming-board-just-sent-65-cease-and-desist-letters-to-sweepstakes-casinos-chumba-fliff-luckyland-and-more-are-on-the-list/)
- Florida's AG sued Stake and VGW on August 19, 2026, calling them "illegal online casinos," and named affiliated payment processors as defendants. [S] — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/08/20/florida-sweeps-casino-lawsuits/); [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/florida-ag-sues-vgw-and-stake-over-sweepstakes-casinos)
- Tennessee's AG announced in late December 2025 that 38 online sweepstakes casinos had been shut down or brought into compliance after letters. [S] — [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/gaming/1729562/tennessee-ag-pushes-illegal-online-sweepstakes-casinos-to-fold)
- New York's AG secured $8 million from VGW (leading sweepstakes operator) in 2026. [S] — [NY AG press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-secures-8-million-leading-operator-illegal-online)
- Market contraction: WOW Vegas ended Sweeps Coin play in Illinois May 1, 2026; Ruby Sweeps exited Indiana May 4, 2026; "several unnamed mid-tier operators have ceased U.S. operations entirely." [F] — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/sweepstakes-casino-bans-2026-state-status)
- Ban bills failed in Minnesota, Florida, Maryland, Massachusetts, Mississippi and Virginia; Idaho, Michigan and Washington rely on existing gambling statutes; there is no federal sweepstakes-ban legislation. [S] — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal)
- Payment and platform layer: Stripe lists "sweepstakes and contests" under prohibited games of chance. [F] — [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses). Google Play permits chance-based rewards only in **non-game** loyalty programs with official rules, disclosed odds, and a genuine separate qualifying purchase. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- A sweepstakes sportsbook launched today must geo-block at least CA, NY, NJ, CT, MT, IN, ME, TN, LA, OK, WA, ID, MI (and realistically IL, FL), which removes well over a third of the US population, and it exposes its payment processor, geolocation vendor and affiliates to liability — so mainstream vendors will not serve a solo-dev sweepstakes product.
- Because bans define the offence by structure (dual currency + simulated gambling + cash prize), renaming currencies does not help; the trend line (9+ statutes in ~18 months, vetoes overridden) suggests more states in 2027.
- Viability verdict for a new solo entrant: not viable as a primary route; legal budget and processor access are prohibitive.

### Gaps
- Nevada SB 256 and the 2025 Louisiana veto named in the brief were not confirmed in sources this session: one tracker still lists Nevada among states where sweepstakes casinos remain available, while Gambling.com lists Louisiana as now banned by statute. Both need primary-source checks.
- New York's bill number is reported as AB 6745 by one tracker and S5935 in the research brief (likely Senate/Assembly companions) — unverified. The Montana effective date (May 12, 2025 per tracker) may be the signing date; verify.
- Gambling Insider's May 2026 monthly update (403) and the Gambling.com article body could not be retrieved, so penalty details for states other than California are missing.

## 4. Prediction markets: CFTC posture, state litigation, DCM vs FCM vs IB, and what third-party developers can build

### Takeaway
The CFTC under Chair Selig is strongly pro-prediction-market (rulemaking that preserves game-outcome sports contracts, suits against nine states), but the courts are split — the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi while the Ninth and Sixth Circuits held sports contracts are not swaps — so sports event contracts are geofenced or under attack in a growing list of states and the issue is headed to the Supreme Court. For developers, becoming a DCM/FCM is out of reach, IB registration is feasible only for a funded company, Kalshi's public API terms forbid third-party front-ends, and the most relevant opening is the CFTC's September 2026 "passive software" no-action relief — which still requires a notice filing and a registered partner willing to share liability.

### Cited Findings
**CFTC posture and rulemaking**
- Jan 29, 2026: CFTC Chair Selig withdrew prior bans/advisories and began drafting comprehensive rules; Feb 17: said CFTC would sue states that interfere with licensed operators; Mar 12: staff advisory on manipulation and prohibited categories. [F] — [RotoWire legal timeline](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- The CFTC (with DOJ in some cases) sued Arizona, Connecticut and Illinois (Apr 2), Wisconsin (Apr 28), Minnesota (May), New Mexico (Jun 12) and Kentucky (Jun 24) — "nine states total" by June 24, 2026. [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- June 10-12, 2026: CFTC published a 267-page NPRM, "Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations," amending Rule 40.11; comments were due July 27, 2026; it followed a March 2026 ANPRM. [S] — [Federal Register, June 12, 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-12/html/2026-11854.htm); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/cftc-proposes-new-rules-for-events-contracts-on-prediction-markets)
- The NPRM largely preserves markets on game outcomes, final scores and win-loss records, but disfavors contracts on player injuries, officiating decisions, discrete in-game actions, physical altercations and pre-collegiate sports; it keeps self-certification. [S] — [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cftc-proposes-new-framework-for-regulating-prediction-markets); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/06/rewriting-the-rulebook-cftc-proposes-rule-changes-for-prediction-market-contracts); reception divided [S] — [Front Office Sports, Sept 25, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/article/cftcs-proposed-sports-rules-wont-quiet-prediction-market-critics/)
- Aug 11, 2026: CFTC invoked emergency authority (CEA § 8a(9)) to keep Kalshi operating nationwide; Aug 14 and Sept 22: review and staff advisory tightening "mention markets." [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- Insider-trading enforcement is real: DOJ/CFTC charged an Army Special Forces soldier (~$409K Polymarket profits, Apr 23, 2026) and a Google engineer (~$1.2M, May 27, 2026). [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)

**Court outcomes (2026)**
- Third Circuit (Apr 6, 2026, 2-1): Kalshi's sports event contracts are swaps on a CFTC-licensed DCM, "so the CFTC has exclusive jurisdiction"; New Jersey cannot enforce. [S] — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/04/06/kalshi-appeals-court-new-jersey/); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/legal/2026/appeals-court-rules-cftc-has-exclusive-jurisdiction-over-event-contracts)
- Ninth Circuit (Aug 28, 2026, unanimous): affirmed dissolution of Kalshi's injunction against Nevada; held sports-event contracts are sports bets, not CFTC-regulated swaps (relying on Rule 40.11's gaming bar); said Kalshi could comply by geofencing Nevada; explicitly split from the Third Circuit. The opinion notes sports contracts were over 90% of Kalshi's 2025 trades. [F] — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/ninth-circuit-kalshi-sports-event-contracts-arent-swaps/)
- Ninth Circuit denied panel rehearing and en banc review (reported Nov 5, 2026), starting Kalshi's 90-day cert clock. [S] — [VitalLaw](https://www.vitallaw.com/news/federal-preemption-9th-cir-ninth-circuit-closes-the-door-on-rehearing-of-kalshi-prediction-markets-ruling/sld0137f603ff1af14c87a178bffdf5e66be5)
- Ninth Circuit, *Blue Lake Rancheria v. Kalshi* (Sept 16, 2026): sports contracts are likely Class III gaming under IGRA on tribal lands. [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- Sixth Circuit (Sept 25, 2026, 3-0): Ohio and Tennessee regulators can pursue Kalshi; sports contracts are not swaps; Kalshi sought en banc rehearing Oct 9. [S]/[F] — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/crypto/kalshi-subject-to-ohio-tennessee-enforcement-after-new-ruling); [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)

**Registration categories and what they take**
- DCM = CFTC-registered exchange (23 core principles; 180-day review once "materially complete"); DCO clears; FCM holds customer funds, must meet net capital under 17 C.F.R. § 1.17 and be an NFA member; **IB** "takes customer orders and makes referrals but does not hold funds or clear trades." [F] — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260415-want-to-get-into-cftc-regulated-event-contract-markets-heres-how-it-works)
- **IB trigger:** registration is generally required for a person who, "for compensation or profit," solicits or accepts orders for event contracts; associated persons must meet proficiency requirements and file Form 8-R; firm files Form 7-R and joins NFA. [F] — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260415-want-to-get-into-cftc-regulated-event-contract-markets-heres-how-it-works)
- KPMG: an IB must route all orders and funds through a registered FCM, register with NFA, and should expect 60-90 days for a complete application; DraftKings launched its predictions app in Dec 2025 as a registered IB routing through CME Group. [S] — [KPMG "Prediction markets: paths to entry"](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/prediction-markets-path-entry.html)
- Distribution is consolidating around brokers/FCMs: Kalshi affiliate Kinetic Markets obtained FCM registration (Mar 24, 2026); Moomoo approved as FCM (May 1); Alpaca registered an FCM; Apex offers an API so brokers/fintechs can add Kalshi contracts; media and gaming firms (e.g., Newsmax Markets) filed for IB registration. [S] — [Finance Magnates on Apex](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/apex-turns-kalshi-into-an-add-on-for-broker-platforms/); [DeFi Rate on broker filings](https://defirate.com/news/new-broker-filings-fuel-distribution-race-prediction-markets/); [KuCoin news on Kinetic](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/kalshi-approved-for-margin-trading-after-affiliate-gets-fcm-registration)

**Building on top of Kalshi / Polymarket**
- **Kalshi Developer Agreement v1.1:** "Use of Kalshi APIs is expressly limited to facilitating a members own trading on the Exchange; all other usages are disallowed." Explicitly prohibited: collecting/caching/aggregating/storing API data except for your own trading and sharing it with third parties without prior written authorization (3.1); "Facilitating trading or account creation by other members, or inducing users to enter their Kalshi password into another site" (3.2); using the API for benchmarking or competitive purposes (3.5); "Sublicensing the API for use by a third party" (3.7). Marketing statements about Kalshi need prior written approval (6.4); Kalshi may terminate at any time at its sole discretion (8). [F] — [Kalshi Developer Agreement PDF](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf)
- **Polymarket Builder Program:** third-party front-ends route user orders by attaching a "builder code" to each signed order; gas-free relayer; public builder leaderboard; tiers govern rate limits. [F] — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/builders/overview.md). Builder fees are additive to platform fees and paid daily; Polymarket can shut off a code for terms violations or integrity concerns. [S] — [Polymarket builder fees docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/builders/fees). A third-party guide says builders can charge up to 1% taker / 0.5% maker (unverified). [S] — [Vinfotech](https://www.vinfotech.com/docs/prediction-market/polymarket-kalshi/polymarket-builder-program)
- April 2026: Polymarket audited Builder Program startups after some marketed copy-trading tools tracking accounts suspected of using non-public information. [S] — [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/polymarket-audits-builders-program-startups-over-insider-trading-concerns/)
- **CFTC "passive software" relief:** Staff Letter 26-09 (Mar 17, 2026, to Phantom Technologies) and Staff Letter 26-25 (Sept 17, 2026) extend no-action relief from IB/AP registration to all providers of software that passively enables customers to transact on CFTC-registered markets — including front-end UIs for viewing market data and submitting orders. Not available if the provider holds assets, generates express "buy"/"sell" signals, or exercises discretion over routing. Ten conditions include: no statutory disqualification, risk disclosures, users onboarded as members/customers of the registered entity, marketing limits, recordkeeping, and the provider **and each registered entity it connects to agreeing to joint and several liability** and CFTC jurisdiction. Not self-executing — requires a notice filing with the Market Participants Division; lasts only until a rulemaking; binds staff, not the Commission. [F] — [Jones Day, Sept 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/cftc-extends-noaction-relief-for-introducing-broker-registration-to-all-providers-of-passive-software)
- Platform friction for prediction-market tooling: Google limited prediction-market ads to CFTC-licensed operators (Jan 7, 2026) and banned prediction-market Chrome extensions effective Aug 1, 2026. [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline). Apple rejected a third-party Polymarket front-end ("Even") under 5.3.4 in Sept 2026, demanding licensing/permission documentation for every selected region. [F] — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/846498)
- Kalshi's consumer refer-a-friend pays about $25 per side in non-withdrawable bonus credit after the friend trades $25, with reported caps of $500-$1,000. [S] — [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/prediction-markets/kalshi/refer-a-friend)

### Inferences
- Building a trading front-end on Kalshi's public API violates the developer agreement on its face; legitimate third-party distribution happens through negotiated broker/FCM/IB arrangements (Robinhood, Apex, Alpaca), not the self-serve API.
- The passive-software relief makes an unregistered front-end legally conceivable, but the joint-and-several-liability condition means the exchange/FCM must sign on — a solo dev needs a commercial partnership plus a notice filing and securities/derivatives counsel. A product that issues picks ("buy/sell signals") or auto-routes falls outside the relief and points toward IB or CTA registration.
- Read-only prediction-market tools (analytics, portfolio trackers using a user's own API key, odds comparison against sportsbooks, alerts) avoid order solicitation, but still face (i) Kalshi's data-sharing prohibition, (ii) Google's Chrome-extension ban, and (iii) Apple treating prediction content as gambling-related.
- A referral/affiliate model paid per funded account risks meeting the IB definition ("solicits ... for compensation"); whether pure advertising qualifies is a lawyer question. Kalshi and Polymarket affiliates clearly exist (promo-code sites), but their contractual and regulatory basis was not found.
- Because the circuit split is unresolved, any product whose value depends on sports event contracts being available nationwide carries binary regulatory risk through at least mid-2027 (Supreme Court timing).

### Gaps
- The CFTC staff letters 26-09 and 26-25 themselves were not opened; all ten conditions and any limits on transaction-based compensation are unknown.
- Whether the Supreme Court has acted on No. 26-299 (sources conflict), whether a final CFTC rule has issued after the July 27, 2026 comment deadline (no source found saying so), and the Fourth Circuit outcome are unconfirmed as of Nov 12, 2026.
- A current state-by-state list of where Kalshi/Polymarket/Robinhood sports contracts are geofenced was not found (confirmed removals: Nevada; court orders in Washington, Michigan, Massachusetts; status in Ohio/Tennessee unclear after the Sixth Circuit ruling).
- IB minimum capital, NFA fee amounts and exam requirements (e.g., Series 3 / swaps proficiency) were not stated in sources opened; no figures are given here.
- Kalshi's and Polymarket US's formal affiliate/partner terms and payouts were not found; I found no source on whether the Polymarket Builder Program applies to the CFTC-regulated Polymarket US venue (QCEX) versus the international crypto venue.
- The Kalshi Developer Agreement PDF is "v1.1" with no visible date; a newer version or separate partner programme may exist.

## 5. Affiliate marketing: licensing/registration and what programs pay

### Takeaway
Affiliate revenue is the most accessible monetization route, but it is regulated in key states: flat-fee/CPA deals generally need only a (cheap or free) vendor registration, while revenue-share deals trigger full vendor/ancillary licensing with background checks. Reported US sportsbook CPAs cluster around $100-$350 per first-time depositor (with wide variance), but top programs are selective and operators are liable for affiliate conduct.

### Cited Findings
- New Jersey DGE: affiliates paid a **flat fee** must apply for vendor registration; sites receiving a **percentage of money wagered or deposited** must be licensed as an ancillary casino service industry enterprise (CSIE); hybrid CPA + revenue-share models also need the ancillary CSIE licence. [S] — [Ifrah Law on NJ DGE affiliate guidance](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/new-jersey-dge-releases-guidance-for-affiliate-marketers/paged-3/2/); [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/law-and-regulation/2014-06-09-new-jersey-dge-announces-affiliate-licensing-policy/)
- NJ fees: initial filing fee for an ancillary CSIE is $2,000; there is no fee for the vendor registration forms (VRF/VRSDF); once a vendor has a registration number via one casino it can contract with others without further filings. [S] — [NJ DGE ancillary/vendor FAQ PDF](https://www.nj.gov/lps/ge/docs/InternetGaming/AncillaryCompaniesVendor.pdf)
- Pennsylvania: flat-fee affiliates must become **registered** gaming service providers; revenue-share affiliates need **certification**; one (older) law-firm article lists $500 for the applicant entity plus $60 per qualifier/affiliated entity. [S] — [Ifrah Law on Pennsylvania](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/how-to-ensure-your-media-affiliate-follows-pennsylvania-law/)
- Colorado: a "vendor major" licence is required for platform providers or anyone sharing in percentage sports betting revenue; a "vendor minor" licence covers other vendors including marketing partners; one affiliate source says CPA affiliates file a simple form and pay a $350 registration fee. [S] — [iGB Affiliate "The licence lowdown"](https://www.igbaffiliate.com/en/articles/regulation-compliance/the-licence-lowdown-which-us-states-are-worth-it-for-affiliates) (single source; page returned HTTP 429 when opened)
- Indiana's Gaming Commission is reported to require vendor licensing for affiliates. [S] — [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/indiana-sports-betting-affiliate)
- General pattern: "in almost every state, sharing directly in the revenue generated from gambling requires ... a far higher level of licensure"; revenue-share licensing can cost "tens of thousands of dollars," with fingerprinting and financial disclosure; key-stakeholder licensing commonly applies at 10% ownership; some states may not require registration for flat media buys. [S] — [iGamingFuture interview with BettingUSA CEO](https://igamingfuture.com/talking-the-licensing-process-in-the-us/); [iGB Affiliate](https://www.igbaffiliate.com/en/articles/strategy/navigating-the-maze-us-market-decoded-for-european-affiliates/)
- State lists conflict across sources: one 2026 operator guide says NJ, NY and MA require affiliate registration "while others do not," which is inconsistent with the Colorado/Indiana/Pennsylvania material above. [S] — [Track360 DFS affiliate playbook](https://track360.io/blog/daily-fantasy-sports-operator-affiliate-playbook-2026)
- No uniform national framework; several jurisdictions require marketing partners to register or be on an approved roster; operators can be sanctioned for affiliates' misleading claims; commercial relationships must be disclosed; "best"/"top-rated" claims increasingly need a stated methodology; bonus headlines must show material terms; some jurisdictions restrict revenue share on self-excluded players. [F] — [DeucesCracked affiliate rules 2026](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/gambling-affiliate-marketing-rules-2026) (general overview, no state specifics)
- CPA benchmarks (vendor blogs, inconsistent): US sportsbook CPA roughly $100-$600 per first-time depositor in one teardown; $120-$350 with a $200 median for tier-1 sportsbooks in another; $50-$300+ elsewhere. DraftKings reported at $100-$300 CPA plus 25-40% revenue share (DraftKings does not publish rates; they vary by state and affiliate cohort); FanDuel reported with tiered CPA from ~$25-$35 per new account plus a 35% revenue-share component with caps; BetMGM $100-$400 per qualifying depositor. [S] — [Track360 sportsbook teardown](https://track360.io/blog/best-sportsbook-affiliate-programs-2026-operator-teardown); [Track360 benchmark](https://track360.io/blog/affiliate-commission-rates-benchmark-2026); [Track360 DraftKings review](https://www.track360.io/blog/draftkings-affiliate-program-operator-review-2026)
- Hybrid deals (smaller CPA + revenue share) overtook pure revenue share in new 2026 contracts (~41%), typically $50-$120 CPA + 15-20% of NGR (iGaming-wide figure). [S] — [Track360 state of affiliate marketing 2026](https://track360.io/blog/state-of-igaming-affiliate-marketing-2026-report)
- Sweepstakes affiliates now carry direct statutory exposure in California: "media affiliates" who knowingly and willfully promote banned sweepstakes games are liable under AB 831. [F] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- Platform rules for affiliate-style apps: Google Play apps may show gambling ads only if compliant with local law, not shown to known under-18s, the app is not in Designed for Families, RG information is displayed, the app provides no simulated gambling and no "gambling support features," and ads are not disguised as buttons; a simulated casino that directs users to real-money casinos is a listed violation. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- Lowest-friction legal path: flat-fee/CPA-only deals (no revenue share), starting in states with free or sub-$500 vendor registration (NJ vendor registration is free; CO ~$350; PA ~$500+), added one state at a time; an LLC is practically required because vendor filings and Apple 5.1.1(ix) both expect a legal entity.
- A solo dev is unlikely to get direct DraftKings/FanDuel deals at launch; realistic routes are affiliate networks/sub-affiliate arrangements or second-tier books, DFS and prediction-market programs — whose licensing treatment is less defined (and for CFTC products raises the IB "solicitation for compensation" question in Section 4).
- CPA figures above are from affiliate-software vendor blogs and should be treated as rough bands, not rate cards.

### Gaps
- Michigan, Tennessee, Virginia, Arizona, Massachusetts and New York affiliate registration specifics and fees were not found; the iGB Affiliate state-by-state article could not be opened (429).
- No reliable source for DFS (PrizePicks, Underdog) or prediction-market (Kalshi, Polymarket) affiliate CPA rates was found; only Kalshi's consumer referral bonus (Section 4).
- Public-company affiliate filings (Gambling.com Group, Better Collective, Catena Media) were not reviewed.
- FTC Endorsement Guide requirements for affiliate disclosure were not sourced this session.

## 6. Apple App Store and Google Play rules

### Takeaway
Both stores gate real-money gambling behind licences, geo-restriction and (on Apple) a legal-entity developer account; neither allows store billing for wagering credits. The practical surprise for solo developers in 2026 is how broadly Apple applies "gambling": free prediction games with prizes, points-only score predictors, tips/odds apps and prediction-market front-ends have all been pushed into the Gambling age rating (18+) or rejected under 5.3.4.

### Cited Findings
**Apple — rule text**
- 5.3 preamble: gambling is "one of the most regulated offerings on the App Store. Only include this functionality if you've fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere you make your app available and are prepared for extra time during the review process." [F] — [Apple Guidelines 5.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.1: "Sweepstakes and contests must be sponsored by the developer of the app." 5.3.2: "Official rules for sweepstakes, contests, and raffles must be presented in the app and make clear that Apple is not a sponsor or involved in the activity in any manner." [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.3: "Apps may not use in-app purchase to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind." [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.4: "Apps that offer real money gaming (such as sports betting, poker, casino games, horse racing) or lotteries must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store. Illegal gambling aids, including card counters, are not permitted on the App Store." [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(ix): apps in "highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges) ... should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.1: unlocking features, subscriptions, in-game currencies and premium content must use in-app purchase; IAP credits may not expire; loot boxes must disclose odds. 3.2.2(viii): "Apps that facilitate binary options trading are not permitted on the App Store." [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 4.7: HTML5/JS mini apps and mini games are allowed if they follow 3.1 for digital goods, are indexed, and have age-restriction mechanisms — the host app is responsible for all such software. [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Apple — age ratings (changed July 2025)**
- Apple replaced 12+/17+ with 13+, 16+ and 18+ (announced July 24, 2025); developers had to complete the new questionnaire by January 31, 2026. [S] — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/apple-overhauls-app-store-age-ratings-adds-new-13-16-and-18-categories-486587-2025-07-28); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/07/25/updated-app-store-age-ratings/)
- "Gambling" = "Betting or wagering using real money or in-game currency that may be exchanged for real money" → **18+**. "Simulated Gambling" = betting "without using real money or in-game currency that can be exchanged for real money" → **13+** if infrequent, **18+** if frequent. "Contests" (skill competitions, trivia, sport contests) → 4+ infrequent / 13+ frequent. Loot boxes → 9+. Regional: Australia rates even infrequent simulated gambling R 18+; Brazil requires an SPA licence for fixed-odds betting apps. [F] — [App Store Connect age ratings reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings)

**Apple — how review actually treats adjacent apps (developer reports, anecdotal)**
- Points-only football score-prediction app (no money, no prizes, 50+ prior approvals) was rejected in June 2026 under 2.3.6 because it includes "tips, tools, predictions or other information related to real money gambling" and was told to answer "Yes" to the **Gambling** (not Simulated Gambling) rating. [F] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 836211](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836211)
- Free football prediction contest with occasional sponsor-funded physical prizes (no entry fee) was rejected in May 2026 under 2.3.6 and 5.3.4, with App Review reportedly characterising it as "real money gambling, real money betting, or real money skill-based gaming" and requesting gambling licences and geo-restrictions; Apple staff only suggested a "Meet with Apple" consultation; another developer noted sweepstakes apps do exist on the store. [F] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 825845](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825845)
- Third-party Polymarket front-end rejected Sept 2026 under 5.3.4 for lacking licensing/permission documentation for every selected country/region; read-only geoblocking did not resolve it in the thread (no replies). [F] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 846498](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/846498)
- A betting-tips app was also rejected under 4.3 (Spam) for code similarity to other apps. [S] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 775936](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775936)

**Google Play**
- "Google Play generally prohibits apps from facilitating real-money gambling and gaming"; exceptions require the developer to be an approved governmental operator or licensed operator, to "complete the application process," and to hold a valid licence for each state and product type (online casino, sports betting, horse racing, lotteries, DFS). [F] — [Google Play real-money gambling policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Approved apps must block under-age users and unlicensed geographies, be free, not use Google Play In-app Billing, be rated AO (Adult Only) or IARC equivalent, and display responsible gambling information. DFS apps require a separate DFS application and must block states where the developer lacks a required licence or DFS is not legal. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Apps that do not qualify may not let users "wager or stake real money for a prize of real-world value," including games that accept money for a chance to win a prize and apps that manage wagers, deposits or winnings; UI prompting users to "BET!" or "REGISTER!" for real-money competition is a violation. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Gamified loyalty: in **game** apps rewards must be at a fixed, documented ratio and "may not be wagered, awarded or exponentiated by game performance or chance-based outcomes"; **non-game** apps may run chance-based rewards/sweepstakes with official rules, disclosed odds, number of winners and deadlines; the qualifying purchase must be a genuine separate transaction. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- A solo developer on an **individual** Apple account cannot ship anything Apple classifies as real-money gaming (5.1.1(ix) + 5.3.4). Forming an LLC and enrolling as an organization (D-U-N-S number) is a prerequisite for anything beyond pure content/tools, and is advisable even for trackers/odds apps because reviewers escalate quickly.
- Expect any app with betting odds, picks, predictions on real matches or sportsbook links to be rated 18+ ("Gambling = Yes") regardless of whether money changes hands; design around this (age gate, RG messaging) rather than fight it.
- Free-to-play prediction contests with prizes are the riskiest "legal" category at App Review: lawful under federal law but repeatedly mis-bucketed as 5.3.4. Mitigations suggested by the threads: official rules in-app naming the developer as sponsor and disclaiming Apple (5.3.1/5.3.2), no gambling vocabulary (bet, odds, stake, payout), no entry fee, limited storefronts, and a pre-submission "Meet with Apple" call. A web app/PWA is the fallback.
- Monetizing picks/analytics subscriptions in an iOS app generally requires IAP under 3.1.1 (Apple's cut), while anything resembling wagering credit can never use IAP (5.3.3) — so paid-entry contests need an external processor that also prohibits them (Section 7).
- On Google Play, the absence of an individual-account rule is moot: only licensed operators pass the application; adjacent apps must avoid any staking flow and follow the gambling-ads conditions.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement was found on bet trackers, odds-comparison apps or apps deep-linking to sportsbooks specifically; such apps clearly exist on the store, but the rule basis is review practice, not text. Forum evidence is anecdotal and unresolved.
- Current Apple rules on US-storefront external purchase links (post-*Epic v. Apple*) for selling picks subscriptions outside IAP were not found in this session.
- Google's application form details, the list of eligible US states/product types, and its policy on prediction-market (CFTC) apps were not retrieved.
- The Korea section of Apple's age-rating page and the remaining text of the guidelines (e.g., 2.3.6 wording) were not read.

## 7. Payments and tax

### Takeaway
Mainstream processors shut the door on exactly the products a solo dev might be tempted to build — Stripe prohibits sweepstakes, contests, fantasy sports with prizes, paid-entry skill games and prize-based sports forecasting — so paid-entry contests need a high-risk merchant account that an unlicensed solo operator will struggle to get. On tax, the OBBBA's 90% cap on gambling-loss deductions is in force for tax year 2026 (repeal efforts unresolved), and the prize-reporting threshold for 1099s rose from $600 to $2,000 from 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe prohibited businesses (gambling section) include: "Games of chance including gambling, internet gambling, casino games, sweepstakes and contests"; "fantasy sports leagues with a monetary or material prize"; "Games of skill including video game and mobile game tournaments or competitions" with a prize; "Payments of an entry or player fee that promise the entrant or player will win a prize of value"; "Sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary or material prize"; lotteries. No "prior approval" pathway appears in these entries; prediction markets and pick-selling are not mentioned explicitly. [F] — [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)
- Apple: IAP cannot be used "to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind" (5.3.3); Google: approved gambling apps may not use Google Play In-app Billing. [F] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Processors are now enforcement targets: California AB 831 makes payment processors liable for knowingly and willfully supporting banned sweepstakes games; Florida's Aug 2026 suits against VGW and Stake named affiliated payment processors. [F]/[S] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/08/20/florida-sweeps-casino-lawsuits/)

### Inferences
- For a solo dev the payments stack effectively dictates the product: subscriptions for content/analytics (IAP on iOS, Stripe on web) are processable; any flow where users pay to enter for a prize is blocked by Stripe's terms and Apple 5.3.3 and would need a specialist high-risk gaming processor that will ask for legal opinions and licences.
- Stripe's "sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary or material prize" entry targets prize contests, not the sale of picks/analytics per se — but the wording is close enough that a tout-subscription business risks account review; confirm with Stripe in writing before launch.
- The 90% loss cap makes high-volume, low-margin betting (arbitrage, +EV grinding, market making) tax-inefficient: a break-even bettor owes tax on 10% of gross losses. This (a) weakens the value proposition of arb/+EV tools for sharp bettors if not repealed, and (b) creates demand for record-keeping/tax-estimation trackers. How the cap applies to CFTC event contracts is a separate, unresolved question for a tax adviser.
- A free-to-play contest operator paying prizes must collect W-9 details and issue 1099s for winners at or above $2,000/year from 2026 — keeping individual prizes small avoids most reporting friction.

## 8. Paid picks / tout services, betting advice, and 2025-2026 advertising and prop-bet restrictions

### Takeaway
Selling picks or betting analysis is largely unregulated speech in the US — no licence is required, and the main legal exposure is deception (false records/guarantees) — which makes it one of the lower legal-risk monetization routes, though platforms treat it as gambling content. Integrity scandals in 2025 produced targeted restrictions on micro-props (MLB's $200 pitch-level cap, CFTC's proposed limits on in-game/injury/officiating contracts) rather than a federal advertising law; the SAFE Bet Act remains stalled.

### Cited Findings
- "Selling picks is a form of free speech, so sports betting touts are legal as long as they don't deceive customers or misrepresent their services"; the industry's lack of regulation leaves verification to consumers. [S] — [RotoWire betting FAQ](https://www.rotowire.com/betting/faq/what-are-sports-betting-touts-1d56d433)
- Nevada considered a bill to register sports handicappers in 2019 (outcome not confirmed). [S] — [KNPR](https://knpr.org/knpr/2019-03/stone-cold-lock-bill-register-sports-handicappers-moving-through-senate)
- Touts commonly run multiple services so that at least one shows a winning record — the classic deception pattern. [S] — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/how-to-bet/dont-buy-picks-from-touts-handicappers-and-pick-sellers/)
- Apple treats "tips, tools, predictions or other information related to real money gambling" as requiring the Gambling age rating. [F] — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836211)
- In the CFTC context, software that "generates express 'buy' or 'sell' signals" falls outside the passive-software relief from IB registration. [F] — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/cftc-extends-noaction-relief-for-introducing-broker-registration-to-all-providers-of-passive-software)
- Polymarket audited builder apps marketing copy-trading of suspected insider accounts (April 2026). [S] — [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/polymarket-audits-builders-program-startups-over-insider-trading-concerns/)
- **MLB micro-bets:** on Nov 10, 2025, after two Cleveland Guardians pitchers were indicted, MLB and sportsbooks representing over 98% of the US market capped pitch-level bets at $200 and removed them from parlays, effective immediately. [S] — [MLB press release](https://www.mlb.com/press-release/press-release-mlb-in-collaboration-with-major-sportsbook-partners-announce-new-limits-on-pitch-level-markets); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/11/10/mlb-sportsbooks-cap-pitch-level-bets/)
- **NBA:** in Oct 2024 some sportsbooks stopped offering "under" props on players with two-way or 10-day contracts; after the 2025 gambling arrests the NBA and NBPA called for limits on player props; on May 1, 2026 the NBA asked the CFTC for limits on league-related contracts. [S]/[F] — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/nba-gambling-arrests-reignite-reform-talk/); [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- **College props:** "many states have banned proposition wagering on college athletics"; the NCAA president asked the CFTC to shut down college sports markets (Jan 13, 2026); Rep. Baumgartner's bill would ban college prop bets federally. [S]/[F] — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/nba-gambling-arrests-reignite-reform-talk/); [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- **CFTC NPRM (June 2026)** would disfavor event contracts on player injuries, officiating decisions, discrete in-game actions, physical altercations and pre-collegiate sports. [S] — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/cftc-proposes-new-rules-for-events-contracts-on-prediction-markets)
- **SAFE Bet Act** (Tonko/Blumenthal; reintroduced March 2025): would restrict advertising, ban prop bets on college/amateur athletes (not pros), and limit AI use; as of mid-2026 it "remains in committee with a low probability of passage in its current form." [S] — [Tonko fact sheet](https://tonko.house.gov/UploadedFiles/SAFE_Bet_fact_sheet_3.25_119th.pdf); [TribalGaming.com analysis, June 2026](https://www.tribalgaming.com/news/safe-bet-act-federal-sports-betting-tribal-compacts-analysis-2026/index.html)
- Sen. Brian Schatz said (late May 2026) he was preparing legislation to outlaw micro prop bets nationally (introduction not confirmed). [S] — [Casinos.com](https://www.casinos.com/us/news/hawaii-senator-pushes-for-federal-ban-on-micro-prop-bets)
- Advertising channels are tightening for prediction markets: the NFL barred prediction-market ads during the Super Bowl (Feb 2, 2026) and Google limits such ads to CFTC-licensed operators. [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- Affiliate/advertising rules were tightened in multiple jurisdictions in 2026 (disclosure, bonus terms, under-age and self-excluded audiences, some influencer channels), with operators held responsible for affiliates' claims. [F] — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/gambling-affiliate-marketing-rules-2026)

### Inferences
- A picks/analysis product's legal risk is mainly consumer-protection (FTC Act § 5 and state UDAP statutes for unsubstantiated win rates or "guaranteed" language) and platform classification — not gambling licensing. Publishing fully tracked, timestamped records is both the differentiator and the legal defence.
- If the picks product also earns affiliate CPAs from the books it recommends, it becomes a state-registered vendor in several states (Section 5) and must disclose the relationship.
- Products built around micro-props, college player props, injuries or officiating are exposed to shrinking supply from both sportsbooks and (prospectively) CFTC exchanges; products around game outcomes, totals and season-long markets are safer.
- On prediction markets, "signals" or copy-trading features push a tool out of passive-software relief and toward IB/CTA territory and platform bans.

## 9. Data and licensing: league data, logos, player names/likeness, official-data mandates, odds scraping

### Takeaway
Facts (scores, stats, player names in a statistical context) have been broadly usable by fantasy operators under court rulings, but use of player names/images in promotional or sportsbook-style contexts has drawn viable right-of-publicity claims, and scraping sportsbook odds violates operators' terms and is being litigated. Official-league-data mandates bind licensed sportsbooks, not adjacent apps, but they shape what licensed data costs.

### Cited Findings
- *Daniels v. FanDuel*: the Indiana Supreme Court held Indiana's right-of-publicity statute "contains an exception for material with newsworthy value that includes online fantasy sports operators' use of college players' information"; the Seventh Circuit then ended the athletes' suit; the court did not foreclose different outcomes under other states' laws. [S] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/fantasy-sports-operators-achieve-big-win-in-indiana-supreme-court/); [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/ruling-daniels-v-fanduel-highlights-value-sports-data-contemporary-culture/)
- *MLBPA (MLB Players Inc.) v. DraftKings* (E.D. Pa.): on March 17, 2025 the court denied DraftKings' motion to dismiss right-of-publicity claims over use of player names and images in sportsbook products/promotion; the case was reported settled in April 2026. [S] — [Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/experience/mlbpis-licensing-fight-ends-in-victory-and-settlement-with-draftkings-70248700); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/mlb-players-union-draftkings-settle-publicity-rights-lawsuit)
- A further MLB-player NIL suit over prop bets was reported in Sept 2026 (details not opened). [S] — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/mlb-nil-lawsuit-draftkings-prop-bets/)
- Commentary describes fantasy/NIL case outcomes as inconsistent across jurisdictions and calls for federal action. [S] — [Pace Law social media blog](https://socialmediablawg.blogs.pace.edu/tag/mlb)
- Leagues monetise official data and marks through exchange deals: MLB signed with Polymarket (Mar 19, 2026); the NBA named Kalshi and Polymarket preferred exchanges and DraftKings received an updated NBA licence covering prediction markets (Oct 26, 2026). [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- Official-data mandates: Illinois requires licensed operators to use official league data for "Tier 2" (in-play/non-final-score) bets unless leagues cannot supply it on "commercially reasonable terms"; Michigan's mandate is triggered only by a league request and operators may petition that terms are not commercially reasonable; Tennessee was first (2019) but later legislation is reported to have dropped the mandate; at least seven states followed Tennessee's model. [S] — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/37240/illinois-sports-betting-rules-commercially-reasonable/); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-tennessee-drop-data-mandate-part-sports-wagering-reshuffle) (sources 2019-2023)
- Odds scraping: Swish Analytics sued OddsJam in California state court (reported Jan 2025), alleging that scraping sportsbook odds violates the sportsbooks' terms even though the odds are publicly visible. [S] — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/01/02/swish-oddsjam-lawsuit/)
- DraftKings' terms of use explicitly prohibit automated scraping (third-party analysis of the provision). [S] — [Conduct Atlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/draftkings/draftkings-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-039378/automated-scraping-of-website-information-prohibited/)
- Licensed odds-API vendors typically prohibit reselling/redistributing raw data or running a competing aggregation service, and grant only revocable, non-transferable licences. [S] — [SharpAPI terms](https://sharpapi.io/terms); [SportsGameOdds terms](https://sportsgameodds.com/terms)
- Kalshi prohibits sharing API data with third parties without written authorization and using it for benchmarking/competitive purposes. [F] — [Kalshi Developer Agreement](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lowest-risk data posture: licence odds from a commercial odds API on a plan that permits display in a consumer app (read the redistribution clause), use text team/player names and statistics only, and avoid player photos, team logos and league marks unless licensed.
- Using athlete names/images to *promote* the product (ads, push notifications, "bet on X" creatives) is where publicity-rights exposure concentrates, per the MLBPA v. DraftKings motion-to-dismiss ruling; statistical/"newsworthy" in-product use is better protected but state-dependent.
- Direct scraping of sportsbooks is a contract/tort risk (and an account-ban risk) rather than a criminal one, but a solo dev cannot afford to be the Swish v. OddsJam test case; a vendor contract shifts the risk.

### Gaps
- Team logo/trademark use (nominative fair use vs. licensing) was not researched; no source captured.
- *C.B.C. Distribution v. MLB Advanced Media* (8th Cir. 2007) was not opened; a search summary characterised it as cutting against the Indiana outcome, which conflicts with my background understanding that it favoured the fantasy operator on First Amendment grounds — verify before citing.
- Outcome of *Swish v. OddsJam* and any 2026 scraping rulings were not found; the details/status of the Sept 2026 MLB NIL prop-bet suit were not confirmed.
- No source on NIL/right-of-publicity claims against prediction markets specifically.

## 10. Age/KYC/geolocation and responsible-gaming obligations for unlicensed adjacent products

### Takeaway
There is no general statute forcing a pure content/tracker app to run KYC or geolocation, but obligations arrive indirectly — through app-store rules (age rating, geo-restriction, RG messaging), affiliate/vendor registration and operator contracts, state ad rules, and vendor-liability provisions in sweepstakes bans. Any product that touches prizes or routes users to wagering should plan for an 18+/21+ age gate, state geofencing and responsible-gaming disclosures from day one.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: real-money gaming apps "must be geo-restricted" to licensed locations; apps with gambling or gambling-related tips/predictions are pushed to the Gambling rating (18+). [F] — [Apple Guidelines 5.3.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Age ratings reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings); [Forum thread 836211](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836211)
- Apple required a Polymarket front-end to limit both App Store availability and in-app access to licensed locations; read-only mode in restricted regions was the developer's attempted fix. [F] — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/846498)
- Google Play: approved gambling apps must prevent under-age use, block unlicensed states, and display responsible gambling information; apps merely showing gambling ads must not show them to known under-18s, must not be in Designed for Families, and must carry RG information on the landing page/listing/in-app. [F] — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- CFTC passive-software relief conditions include publishing risk disclosures, onboarding users as members/customers of the registered entity (i.e., the registered entity performs KYC), marketing limits and recordkeeping. [F] — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/cftc-extends-noaction-relief-for-introducing-broker-registration-to-all-providers-of-passive-software)
- California AB 831 extends liability to geolocation providers, payment processors and media affiliates that knowingly support banned sweepstakes games — i.e., serving California users is itself the violation, making accurate geoblocking a legal necessity for that model. [F] — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- The Ninth Circuit noted Kalshi could comply with Nevada law by geofencing Nevada users — courts treat state geofencing as a baseline expectation. [F] — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/ninth-circuit-kalshi-sports-event-contracts-arent-swaps/)
- Affiliate marketing rules restrict marketing to under-age audiences and self-excluded individuals and require clear disclosure of commercial relationships and bonus terms; operators are responsible for affiliates' conduct. [F] — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/gambling-affiliate-marketing-rules-2026)
- Prize contests: official rules must be presented in-app (Apple 5.3.2); from 2026, prize payments of $2,000+ trigger 1099 reporting, which requires collecting winner identity/TIN. [F]/[S] — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/tax-authorities/1754700/new-prize-tax-rules-raise-the-1099-threshold-for-sweepstakes-and-contests)

### Inferences
- Practical minimum for an adjacent product: self-declared age gate (21+ if linking to sportsbooks, since most states set sports betting at 21 — background knowledge, not sourced here), state-aware content (only show offers for books licensed in the user's state), problem-gambling helpline messaging (e.g., 1-800-GAMBLER), clear "not a sportsbook" and affiliate disclosures.
- For free-to-play prize contests: official rules with eligibility by state and age, "no purchase necessary," void-where-prohibited states, and identity verification only at prize redemption.
- Full KYC/geolocation vendors (IDV, GeoComply-type services) are needed only once money stakes exist; their cost and their own vendor-liability concerns are another reason paid-entry models are impractical for a solo dev.

### Gaps
- No source captured for state minimum betting ages, state-mandated responsible-gaming language for affiliates, or state sweepstakes registration/bonding requirements for large prize pools (from background knowledge, New York, Florida and Rhode Island have such rules — unverified this session).
- COPPA/state privacy-law implications and the new state app-store age-verification laws were not researched.

## 11. Summary: tiered list of product types from lowest to highest legal risk for a solo developer

### Takeaway
Risk rises in step with three things: money flowing from users for a chance at a prize, the developer touching wagers or orders, and dependence on legal theories under active state attack. Content/analytics/trackers and affiliate-monetised tools are the realistic solo-dev zone; free-to-play prize contests are lawful but platform-fragile; everything involving paid entry, redeemable currency, P2P money or order routing requires licences, registrations or partners a solo developer cannot practically obtain.

### Cited Findings
- Federal law expressly excludes contests where participants stake nothing but effort or free sponsor-provided points. [F] — [31 U.S.C. § 5362(1)(E)(viii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5362)
- Apple nonetheless rejected or re-rated free prediction apps (with and without prizes) as gambling in May-June 2026. [F] — [Thread 825845](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825845); [Thread 836211](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836211)
- Pick-selling is legal speech absent deception. [S] — [RotoWire FAQ](https://www.rotowire.com/betting/faq/what-are-sports-betting-touts-1d56d433)
- Flat-fee/CPA affiliates need vendor registration in states such as NJ (no fee) and PA; revenue share requires higher licensure. [S] — [NJ DGE FAQ](https://www.nj.gov/lps/ge/docs/InternetGaming/AncillaryCompaniesVendor.pdf); [Ifrah Law (PA)](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/how-to-ensure-your-media-affiliate-follows-pennsylvania-law/)
- Stripe prohibits paid-entry prize contests, prize fantasy sports, sweepstakes and prize-based sports forecasting. [F] — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)
- Dual-currency sweepstakes are banned by statute in roughly 9-12 states with vendor liability and active AG litigation. [S]/[F] — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- Kalshi's API terms bar facilitating others' trading; CFTC passive-software relief requires a notice filing and joint liability with a registered entity; soliciting/accepting orders for compensation requires IB registration. [F] — [Kalshi Developer Agreement](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/cftc-extends-noaction-relief-for-introducing-broker-registration-to-all-providers-of-passive-software); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260415-want-to-get-into-cftc-regulated-event-contract-markets-heres-how-it-works)
- Circuit split on sports event contracts (3rd Cir. for Kalshi; 9th and 6th Cir. for states). [F] — [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)
- Sportsbook operator fees run from $50k applications to $1M (NC) and $25M (NY) per state. [S] — [ncgaming.gov](https://ncgaming.gov/?p=8424); [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/online-sports-betting/wagering-tax-rates-us/)

| 11 | **P2P real-money betting** (social/"friends" betting with developer holding funds or taking a fee) | Likely gambling/money-transmission licences; funded peers moved to CFTC DCM registration | Social-gambling exemptions generally require no house profit; funds custody; platforms prohibit | Fees/rake | Yes; not advisable |
| 12 (highest) | **Operating a sportsbook** or a DCM/FCM | State operator licences ($50k-$25M per state) or CFTC registration (23 core principles, capital) | Out of reach for a solo developer | Hold/fees | N/A |

Additional synthesis points:
- Lowest-risk route to market: web app first (avoids App Review classification risk), iOS app as a companion once an LLC and organization developer account exist; start Tier 1-3, add Tier 4 CPA affiliate state by state.
- Lowest-risk monetization stack: subscription for tools/content + CPA/flat-fee affiliate (no revenue share) + ads; avoid any user payment that is, or looks like, an entry fee for a prize.
- A "P2P among friends, no money handled by the app" design (score-keeping of friendly bets settled offline) sits near Tier 3 rather than Tier 11, provided the app never holds funds, takes no cut and avoids facilitating payment — but Apple may still treat it as gambling-related; this distinction needs a lawyer's confirmation.
- Biggest regulatory swing factors to monitor through 2027: Supreme Court action on the prediction-market circuit split (No. 26-299 and Kalshi's expected Ninth Circuit petition), the CFTC final rule on event contracts, a rulemaking replacing the passive-software no-action relief, further state sweepstakes bans, and whether Congress repeals the 90% loss-deduction cap.
