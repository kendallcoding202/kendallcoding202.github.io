# Answering the channel question with paid search

The open question from `market.md` was whether these contractors search at all,
or whether they find software through their GC, their bookkeeper, or a trade
association. Since paid search is the intended primary channel, that question has
to be answered before real money goes into it.

## What the evidence says so far

**Suggestive, not conclusive: a large content-marketing ecosystem exists.**
Projul, Kaster, Steph's Books, Payroll4Construction, LumberFi, Fingercheck,
eBacon, and Prevailing Wage Consulting all publish certified-payroll guides
aimed at contractors; Payroll4Construction runs an entire series. Companies do
not fund SEO content for queries nobody types. Kaster's post is titled *"I'm a
Subcontractor and It's My First Time Dealing with Certified Payrolls. Where Do I
Start?"* — that is a long-tail search query wearing a headline.

**Also suggestive: multiple vendors compete commercially** for the same
audience — Points North, Certified Payroll Pro, eMars, Passport Workforce,
B2Gnow. Sustained competition implies a reachable market.

**What this does not prove.** Content demand is *informational* intent. It does
not establish that the searcher is a small subcontractor with budget authority,
rather than a bookkeeper, an estimator, a GC's compliance clerk, or a student.
The gap between "people search this" and "buyers search this" is exactly where
ad budgets die. It has to be measured, not assumed.

## The finding that should shape the campaign

General contractors own the compliance chain and are penalized for their
subcontractors' violations, so GCs collect, review, and **reject** subs'
payrolls. That rejection is the trigger event. A sub who has just been told
their payroll is wrong — with payment held up behind it — is a person with a
problem, a deadline, and a reason to buy today.

That is worth far more than the generic category term. Bid the moment, not the
category.

## Keyword tiers

**Tier 1 — the trigger. Start here.** Cheap, specific, high intent, and almost
nobody bids on it because it does not look like a software query.

> certified payroll rejected · WH-347 rejected · GC says my certified payroll is
> wrong · certified payroll fringe benefits wrong · do I have to pay fringe in
> cash · certified payroll overtime calculation · how to fill out WH-347 ·
> certified payroll mistakes · wrong classification certified payroll

**Tier 2 — the category.** Expensive and contested by funded incumbents. Do not
open here.

> certified payroll software · prevailing wage software · Davis-Bacon compliance
> software · certified payroll reporting

**Tier 3 — competitor-adjacent.** Only once Tier 1 economics are proven.

> LCPtracker alternative · Certified Payroll Pro alternative · certified payroll
> software for small contractors

## The test

**Step 0 — free, before spending anything.** Open a Google Ads account and use
Keyword Planner. It returns volume ranges and real CPC estimates per term. An
afternoon here answers "do they search, and what does it cost" for zero dollars,
and may kill the channel outright. Do this first.

**Step 1 — roughly $1,000 over three to four weeks.** Tier 1 only. Exact and
phrase match, no broad match. Geo-limit to two or three states with heavy
federally funded construction. Point every ad at the free checker with the
headline matched to the query.

### The decision rule, and why it is not cost-per-customer

At $79/month with even modest retention, lifetime value lands well over $1,000,
so a customer acquisition cost of $200–300 would be perfectly healthy. The
trouble is arithmetic: $1,000 at a $6 click buys about 165 clicks, which yields
maybe 5 signups. Five is not a number anyone can draw a conclusion from.

So judge the small test on **cost per completed free check** instead — a metric
the budget can actually resolve:

| Result | Read |
|---|---|
| Under $25 per completed check | Working. Scale the budget and start measuring conversion properly. |
| $25–60 per completed check | Ambiguous. Fix landing page or targeting, run once more before deciding. |
| Over $60, or clicks that never start a check | The channel is wrong, or the audience is not who we think. Stop. |

A high click-through with no completed checks is the most informative failure
available: it means people search but the pasted-payroll format is too much
friction — a product problem, not a channel one, and worth knowing early.

## What still needs building for the test to be measurable

The checker is currently anonymous end to end, which is right for privacy and
useless for measurement. Before spending on ads it needs:

1. **Funnel events** — landed, started a check, completed a check, clicked
   through to save. Without these there is no denominator.
2. **A "save this project" capture.** The single best intent signal, and it can
   exist before the paid product does: someone who hands over an email to avoid
   retyping rates next week is a real prospect.

**A caveat that must not be glossed over.** The landing page currently promises
"Nothing is saved," and that promise is doing real work — it is why a contractor
will paste payroll containing worker names into a stranger's website. Adding
email capture must not quietly break it. The honest version keeps payroll data
unstored and says so precisely: *we never store your payroll; if you give us an
email we store that*. Blurring the two would be the kind of thing that destroys
trust in a small market where contractors talk to each other.
